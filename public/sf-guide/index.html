A Guide

San Francisco

A handful of corners of the city worth wandering to — museums, galleries, a cathedral on a hill, the waterfront, and a few good places to eat. Nothing here needs to be done in order. Drift in as the morning suggests.

Golden Gate Park & the de Young

A morning unhurried among American painting, contemporary photography, and an observation tower that rises above the trees to the Pacific. The Music Concourse outside makes for a slow walk between visits.

  1. de Young Museum

    50 Hagiwara Tea Garden Dr

    An anchor of the city's fine-arts collection — American painting from the seventeenth century forward, contemporary photography, textiles, and a program of rotating exhibitions. The free Hamon Tower observation level opens onto one of the calmest 360° views in the city.

    Closed Mondays. The Herzog & de Meuron building is itself worth the visit.

    Open in Maps

An unhurried café sits inside the museum.

Lincoln Park & the Legion of Honor

A Beaux-Arts pavilion on a clifftop above the Pacific, with Rodin galleries, European masters, and a strong holding of prints and drawings. A short walk from the entrance, George Segal's Holocaust Memorial sits quietly in a grove.

  1. Legion of Honor

    100 34th Ave

    The building is modeled on the Hôtel de Salm in Paris; the collection spans medieval through nineteenth-century European painting, decorative arts, and one of the world's largest holdings of Rodin sculpture.

    Closed Mondays. A café on the lower level handles a quiet lunch.

    Open in Maps

  2. Holocaust Memorial

    Lincoln Park, just outside the Legion of Honor

    George Segal's white-painted bronze installation, set in a small grove northeast of the museum. Free, outdoors, and best approached on foot from the Legion's entrance.

    Open in Maps

SFMOMA & Yerba Buena

The city's modern and contemporary axis. SFMOMA anchors the block; the Museum of the African Diaspora sits across the street; Berggruen, a long-standing dealer in modern painting, is around the corner. Yerba Buena Gardens between them offers a pause.

  1. SFMOMA

    151 3rd St

    Seven floors of modern and contemporary art with deep holdings in postwar painting, photography, and design, and rotating exhibitions on the upper levels. The 2016 Snøhetta expansion opened public stairways and free ground-floor galleries.

    Closed Tuesdays and Wednesdays. The photography wing is among the strongest on the West Coast.

    Open in Maps

  2. Museum of the African Diaspora

    685 Mission St

    Three floors of contemporary art of the African diaspora inside the St. Regis tower. Smaller and more focused than SFMOMA, easily absorbed in an hour.

    Closed Mondays.

    Open in Maps

  3. Berggruen Gallery

    10 Hawthorne St

    A long-established San Francisco gallery for modern and contemporary painting, around the corner from SFMOMA. An understated, well-lit space that makes for a quiet final stop.

    Weekday hours; worth a look at the current show before walking over.

    Open in Maps

SFMOMA has cafés on-site; Yerba Buena Gardens has casual options between buildings.

Civic Center & the Performing Arts

The civic spine of the city — the Asian Art Museum's collections by day, and four performing-arts halls within a three-block walk for the evening. Hayes Valley sits a block west for an unhurried dinner before a show.

  1. Asian Art Museum

    200 Larkin St

    One of the largest collections of Asian art in the West, set inside the old Beaux-Arts main library and reworked by Gae Aulenti. Strong holdings in Chinese painting, Japanese ceramics, and South and Southeast Asian sculpture.

    Closed Tuesdays and Wednesdays. Late hours on Thursdays.

    Open in Maps

  2. Davies Symphony Hall

    201 Van Ness Ave

    Home of the San Francisco Symphony.

    Open in Maps

  3. War Memorial Opera House

    301 Van Ness Ave

    San Francisco Opera and San Francisco Ballet perform here.

    Open in Maps

  4. SFJAZZ Center

    201 Franklin St

    A purpose-built jazz hall with a year-round concert calendar.

    Open in Maps

Dogpatch

A working waterfront neighborhood that has, over a decade, become San Francisco's quietest gallery district. Two stops sit a few blocks apart, and the eating is among the best in the city for an unfussy lunch or a morning pastry.

  1. Minnesota Street Project

    1275 Minnesota St

    A purpose-built complex housing eleven contemporary galleries and rotating exhibitions under one roof. The density of programming makes for an efficient hour — a real survey of what the city's commercial scene is doing.

    Galleries free and open Tue–Sat, 11–5; individual hours vary.

    Open in Maps

  2. Museum of Craft and Design

    2569 3rd St

    A small museum of contemporary craft, design, and material art, with one or two thoughtful exhibitions on at any time. The scale is exactly right — one floor, a few rooms, an after-coffee visit.

    Closed Monday and Tuesday.

    Open in Maps

Neighbor Bakehouse — 2343 3rd St — morning pastries and coffee until 2 pm, Wed–Sun. Open in Maps
Piccino — 1001 Minnesota St — Italian, vegetarian options, for lunch or an early dinner. Open in Maps

Nob Hill & Chinatown Galleries

Begin atop Nob Hill at Grace Cathedral, then descend through the edge of Chinatown to two of the city's strongest commercial galleries — one on Grant, one on Geary.

  1. Grace Cathedral

    1100 California St

    The Episcopal cathedral, modeled in part on Notre-Dame de Paris, with a Ghiberti reproduction of the Gates of Paradise, Keith Haring's altarpiece in the AIDS Interfaith Chapel, and two labyrinths — one inside, one in the forecourt.

    Open daily; the interior is cool, quiet, and built for slow walking.

    Open in Maps

  2. Jessica Silverman

    621 Grant Ave

    A contemporary gallery on the Chinatown–Financial District border with a strong roster of mid-career artists. Crisply curated; a short, satisfying visit.

    Tue–Sat, 10–6.

    Open in Maps

  3. Fraenkel Gallery

    49 Geary St, Suite 450

    A canonical photography gallery — Diane Arbus, Lee Friedlander, Hiroshi Sugimoto, Adam Fuss are part of the program — set inside 49 Geary, a building of perhaps a dozen galleries on its upper floors, all worth a wander.

    Tue–Fri 10:30–5:30, Sat 11–5.

    Open in Maps

The Marina & Fort Mason

Walk the colonnade of the Palace of Fine Arts, then drift east along the bay to Fort Mason — a former military complex turned arts campus on the water — with one of the city's best vegetarian dining rooms beside it.

  1. Palace of Fine Arts

    3301 Lyon St

    Bernard Maybeck's 1915 rotunda and colonnade, restored, with a small lagoon and lawns to walk. A piece of architectural theater that rewards slow circumambulation.

    Outdoor grounds free and open daily.

    Open in Maps

  2. Fort Mason Center for Arts & Culture

    2 Marina Blvd

    A historic waterfront campus housing a constellation of arts organizations, including FOR-SITE Foundation, with rotating exhibitions and installations across the buildings.

    Worth checking the current programming before going.

    Open in Maps

Greens Restaurant — 2 Marina Blvd — landmark vegetarian dining room founded by the SF Zen Center in 1979, with a wall of bay window. Reservations advisable. Open in Maps

The Ferry Building & Sausalito

A waterfront half-day. Begin in the Ferry Building's marketplace; cross the bay to Sausalito on a Golden Gate ferry; come back on a later sailing.

  1. Ferry Building

    1 Ferry Building, The Embarcadero

    The 1898 ferry terminal, restored as a public marketplace — coffee, pastries, oysters, ceramics at Heath, tea at Imperial Tea Court, books, flowers. The Saturday farmers' market is the busiest and most rewarding day.

    Building open daily 7 am–10 pm; individual shop hours vary.

    Open in Maps

  2. Sausalito

    Golden Gate Ferry from the Ferry Building

    A twenty-five-minute crossing with full views of the bay and the bridge. In Sausalito, walk Bridgeway, browse the small galleries along the waterfront, and take a later ferry home.

    Check the Golden Gate Ferry schedule the day of — sailings are limited.

    Open in Maps

Many cafés inside the Ferry Building marketplace itself.

A Drive Down the Peninsula

Thirty miles south, set into the hills above Woodside, a 1917 country estate with sixteen acres of formal gardens. A whole day, restful pace, and the most rewarding garden visit within reach of the city.

  1. Filoli

    86 Cañada Rd, Woodside

    The Bourn family's Georgian-revival house and an estate of Italianate gardens — a sunken garden, a knot garden, a long pool flanked by yews, an orchard. A National Trust site.

    Open daily 10–5; tickets in advance via filoli.org. About 45 minutes by car from the city.

    Open in Maps

A café on the grounds; Woodside and Menlo Park also have lunch options.
