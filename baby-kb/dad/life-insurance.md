# Life Insurance for New Parents

## The decision frame

Life insurance protects against the financial consequence of premature death — replacement of income, payoff of obligations, funding of children's expenses to adulthood. With a newborn, this is when the protection is most important; the term length should cover the dependency period.

## Term vs whole life

For 95% of families, **term life insurance** is the correct product. Reasons:
- Premium is dramatically lower (10–20× cheaper for equivalent death benefit).
- The cash-value buildup of whole life is an inferior-return investment vs. term + invest-the-difference.
- Whole life is sold heavily because the commission is high; this is not aligned with the buyer's interest.

**Whole life makes sense in narrow cases**: estate-tax planning at very high net worth (>$15M+ per spouse), specific business-continuity planning, or special-needs trust funding. None of these is the typical first-baby case.

## Term length

For new parents (baby born May 2026):

- **20-year term** ends in 2046. Baby is age 20. Most college years covered if parent dies during term.
- **30-year term** ends in 2056. Baby is age 30. Full dependency covered + buffer.
- **30-year term is the right answer for new parents** unless premium is prohibitive.

Premium difference: 30-year is ~50% more expensive than 20-year. For a healthy 30–40-year-old non-smoker, $500K–1M coverage on 30-year term costs ~$30–80/month. Worth it.

## Coverage amount

Three frameworks:

### Income replacement

Annual gross income × 10–15. For a $300K earner: $3M–$4.5M.

### Need-based

Sum of:
- Mortgage payoff.
- 18 years of childcare + living expenses (typical $25K–60K/year × 18).
- College: 4 × $90K (in 2026 dollars) ≈ $400K-700K inflated.
- Retirement gap if surviving parent's earnings won't cover.

### DIME

Debt + Income (10x) + Mortgage + Education. Sum the four; sanity-check against income-replacement.

For a typical Bay Area / NYC dual-earner couple with one young child, **$1.5M–$3M per parent** is the typical right answer. Both parents covered, not just the higher earner — the homemaker/lower earner's death also creates substantial economic loss (childcare replacement alone is $50K–80K/year).

## Buy from whom

**Direct online** (Haven Life, Bestow, Ladder, Ethos): instant or near-instant approval, competitive rates, fewer health questions for healthy applicants. Good for healthy 28–40-year-olds.

**Independent broker** (Term4Sale, AccuQuote, Quotacy): best for shopping multiple insurers' rates. Useful if you have any health flag (BMI, family history, treated condition).

**Direct from carrier** (Banner Life, Pacific Life, Protective, Mutual of Omaha): cheaper for some risk profiles but requires more legwork.

**Avoid**: commission-driven advisor pushing whole life as "permanent protection" or "tax-free retirement vehicle." If the agent makes whole life sound clearly better, change agents.

## Underwriting

- **Medical exam** typically required for >$500K coverage. Blood draw, urine sample, BP, height/weight.
- **Health questionnaire** covers family history (cancer, heart disease before 60), personal medical history, mental health treatment, prescription medications.
- **Lifestyle questions**: smoking, drug use, alcohol, hazardous hobbies (skydiving, scuba past certain depths).
- **Credit / financial check** for very large policies.

Smoking (including any in last 12 months, including marijuana in some states) increases premium 2–3×.

## Timing

The best time to buy life insurance is **before the baby is born** when both parents are at their healthiest and pregnancy hasn't introduced new health flags. Second-best is the first month postpartum.

If you haven't bought yet:
- **Apply now**.
- Don't wait to "lose the baby weight" or "wait until physicals are normal" — applying with current health is faster, and underwriting gives you the result.

## Beneficiary structure

- **Primary**: spouse.
- **Contingent**: trust for children (NOT children directly — minors can't receive insurance proceeds; assets go through court/guardian-ad-litem). The revocable living trust your estate attorney sets up is the correct contingent beneficiary. See `dad/estate-guardianship.md`.

## Riders

- **Waiver of premium**: if you become disabled, premiums waived. Often worthwhile.
- **Accelerated death benefit**: if diagnosed with terminal illness, can access portion of death benefit while alive. Usually included free.
- **Conversion option**: convert term to permanent without re-underwriting. Useful if health declines mid-term.
- **Child rider**: small coverage on children. Generally not worth it — inexpensive separately, and the financial loss from child death is not the same.

## What about employer-provided insurance?

- Most employers offer 1–2× salary in group term life. **Never count this as your primary coverage** — it's not portable, terminates when employment ends, and is typically insufficient.
- Use it as supplement on top of your individual policy.
