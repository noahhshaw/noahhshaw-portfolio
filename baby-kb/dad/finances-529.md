# 529 Plan — Setup and Strategy

## Why 529 specifically

Among college-savings options, the 529 plan dominates on tax efficiency for tuition use:
- **Tax-free growth** when used for qualified education expenses.
- **State income tax deduction** in 30+ states for in-state-plan contributions.
- **Federal gift-tax exclusion** allows $19,000/year per donor (2026), or 5-year frontload of $95,000 in one year.
- **Roth IRA rollover (post-2024)**: unused 529 funds can roll to a Roth IRA in the beneficiary's name (15-year holding period, $35,000 lifetime limit, subject to annual Roth limits).
- **K-12 tuition use**: up to $10,000/year per beneficiary for K-12 private school tuition (federal level; some states don't conform — check state rules).
- **Student loan repayment**: up to $10,000 lifetime.

Compared to UTMA: 529 has tax advantages and parent control; UTMA hands assets to child at age of majority (18 or 21 depending on state). For education-targeted savings, 529 wins decisively. For flexible savings, UTMA is more open-ended.

## Plan selection — direct-sold low-cost

Top direct-sold 529 plans by Morningstar/SavingForCollege rankings (year over year, the same 5–8 plans cluster at top):

- **Utah my529** (Vanguard funds, low ER 0.10–0.20%, no state-residence requirement).
- **New York 529 Direct Plan** (Vanguard funds, state tax deduction for NY residents).
- **California ScholarShare 529** (TIAA-CREF + Vanguard + DFA, no state tax deduction for CA residents but federally-equivalent).
- **Nevada Vanguard 529**.
- **Illinois BrightStart Direct**.
- **Massachusetts U.Fund**.

**Avoid advisor-sold plans** unless your fee-only advisor specifically recommends one — typically 1–2% expense load that compounds badly over 18 years.

## How much to contribute

Three benchmarks:

### Benchmark 1: full in-state public

4-year in-state public university 2026 cost ~$30,000/year × 4 = $120,000. Inflated to 2044 at 4% annual cost growth ≈ $245,000.

To save $245,000 in 18 years at 6% real return: ~$650/month from birth.

### Benchmark 2: full elite private (today's cost)

2026 Ivy/equivalent COA ~$90,000/year × 4 = $360,000. Inflated to 2044 at 4% ≈ $735,000.

To save $735,000 in 18 years at 6%: ~$1,950/month from birth.

### Benchmark 3: maximum tax-favored contribution

Front-load $95,000 per parent (5-year frontload of annual gift exclusion) in year 1 = $190,000 total. Then nothing more from parents; relies on growth + relatives' contributions.

At 6% real return over 18 years, $190,000 → ~$543,000.

## Strategy

For two high-earning parents with high-achievement-oriented goals:

**Recommended baseline**: Open with $5,000–10,000 initial contribution at SSN receipt (week 3–4). Auto-debit $1,500–2,000/month.

**If using 5-year frontload**: contribute $95,000 in year 1. Notify CPA (filing requires Form 709 to elect 5-year averaging). Spouse can do same — total $190,000.

**Investment selection**: age-based glide path is the default and reasonable. "Aggressive" track for first 8–10 years (90%+ equity), shifting to balanced by age 14, conservative by age 17.

**Grandparent-owned 529s**: previously had financial-aid penalty. The FAFSA Simplification Act (effective 2024–2025) eliminated grandparent 529s from FAFSA reporting. Grandparent-owned 529s are now financial-aid-friendly. Encourage if family is willing.

## Mechanics

1. **Open at**: my529.org (Utah), NY529.org, scholarshare529.com (CA), or your state's plan portal.
2. **Required**: parent SSN, baby SSN, bank account for funding.
3. **Time to open**: 1–2 weeks for SSN issuance after birth, ~30 min for plan setup.
4. **Auto-debit setup** for monthly contribution.
5. **Gift portal**: most plans have a gift link grandparents and friends can use directly without going through your account.

## Other education-savings vehicles

- **UTMA / UGMA**: flexible but hands to child at age of majority. Useful for non-education savings (cars, weddings, down payments).
- **Coverdell ESA**: $2,000/year max, K-12 + college. Mostly superseded by 529 K-12 expansion.
- **Roth IRA in parent's name**: contributions accessible without penalty; growth penalty-free for college if needed. Requires earned income; subject to Roth income limits.
- **Trump Account / Child Savings Account** (if applicable in 2026 legislation): track separately — additional vehicle complementary to 529.
- **Taxable brokerage in parent's name**: most flexible; least tax-efficient.

## What NOT to do

- **Don't put money in baby's name** in a UTMA without a specific reason — counts heavily against financial aid (20% of UTMA assets vs. 5.64% of parent assets in FAFSA SAI calculation).
- **Don't put 529 in grandparent's name unless they are the primary funder** — lose control of plan.
- **Don't over-fund** beyond reasonable estimate — early withdrawal for non-qualified expenses incurs income tax + 10% penalty on growth. Roth rollover provides escape valve up to $35,000/lifetime per beneficiary.

## Ongoing

- **Annual review**: contribution level, allocation, state tax filing.
- **Beneficiary change**: 529 beneficiary can change to any "qualifying family member" without tax — siblings, cousins, nieces/nephews, even self.
- **Pull-out timing**: closer to college, shift to conservative; expense planning at high school years.
