# Extracurriculars by Age

## Frame

The "tiger" instinct to enroll early can be operationalized correctly or incorrectly. Done well, early extracurriculars build foundational competencies (motor, music, language, social), maintain access to top programs that have waitlists, and establish routines. Done poorly, they overstimulate, create parental burnout, and substitute structured activity for the messier free-play that drives executive function and self-direction.

The empirical sweet spot in year 1: **2–3 enrichment activities per week**, embedded in routine, with substantial unstructured floor/play time as the dominant mode.

## Year 1 (months 0–12)

### Recommended

- **Music class** (Music Together, Kindermusik): from 6 weeks. Once/week. Active singing/movement with parent. Highest-evidence intervention (Trainor 2012).
- **Library storytime**: free, weekly, 0–18 mo programs. Reading exposure + parent socialization.
- **Parent-baby swim**: from 3–6 mo. Once/week. Less about swimming skill (not developmentally meaningful in year 1), more about water comfort, safety, parent bonding.
- **Suzuki listening exposure**: from 6–12 mo. Daily, passive (recordings) — pre-cursor to formal lessons in years 3–4.
- **Daily reading aloud**: 30+ minutes by month 6.

### Optional / additional

- **Parent-baby yoga**: low effort, parent benefit + baby exposure.
- **Sensory play class** (e.g., "messy play"): once/week. Limited evidence of outcomes; fun.
- **Bilingual playgroup**: if the family is raising bilingual.

### Avoid

- **Structured "academic" classes** (flashcards, baby gym with curriculum) — no evidence of benefit, opportunity cost vs. unstructured play.
- **Screen-based "learning" programs** for under-2.

## Year 2 (months 12–24)

### Recommended

- **Music** continued. Music Together transitions to Kindermusik or program-specific Suzuki ECE.
- **Gym/movement** (My Gym, The Little Gym): from 12–18 mo. Once/week.
- **Library storytime**: continues.
- **Outdoor / nature class**: from 18 mo (Tinkergarten or similar).
- **Bilingual playgroup**: continues if relevant.

### Optional

- **Swimming** beginning to be more skill-developing; from 18 mo.
- **Parent-toddler ballet** (Tutu School-type): cute, low-evidence.

## Year 3 (months 24–36)

### Recommended

- **Music**: Suzuki Early Childhood Education (SECE) classes if continuing toward instrument lessons.
- **Gym/movement**: once/week.
- **Preschool** begins (typically 2s for September after age 2, or 3s).
- **Swimming**: structured group lessons at age 3.

### Optional

- **Soccer Shots / Soccer Tots**: age 2.5+. Mostly running, sometimes ball.
- **Rhythmic / pre-ballet** (Tutu School, others): age 2+.
- **Foreign-language immersion**: if not at home, formal classes start age 2–3.

## Year 4 (months 36–48)

### Recommended

- **Suzuki violin or piano lessons** typically begin age 3.5–4. 30–45 min weekly + group class. Daily home practice 5–15 min.
- **Preschool**: 3s program, 3–5 days/week.
- **Swimming**: continuing skill-building.

### Optional

- **Soccer (age 4+)** organized.
- **Martial arts** (taekwondo, karate) typically age 4+.
- **Theater / drama**: age 4+.

## Year 5+ (school age)

The framework expands; see separate planning. Key principles:

- **One musical instrument** continued seriously.
- **One physical sport** (team or individual; varies by family / child).
- **One "intellectual" activity** (chess, math circle, debate — depending on age).
- **Substantial free/unstructured time** preserved.

## Decision principles

1. **Don't outsource what parents do best.** Reading aloud, playing on the floor, conversations during walks, family dinner — these are the highest-ROI interventions and don't require enrollment.
2. **Use enrichment classes for what families don't replicate at home.** Group musical experience with peers, swim safety, multi-sensory class formats.
3. **Don't over-schedule.** A 1-year-old with 5 weekly classes is overstimulated; a 4-year-old with 7 activities has no time for free play.
4. **Watch for child's signals.** Forced enrichment early in life is associated with later disengagement; child-directed mastery (Suzuki has this built in via parental modeling) sustains motivation.
5. **Maintain access** by being on waitlists for the high-demand programs (top Suzuki teachers, top language immersion, top preschool feeders).

## What the evidence supports

- **Music**: active group classes (Trainor 2012) → modest cognitive + social outcomes.
- **Reading aloud**: dialogic reading (Whitehurst 1988) → expressive vocabulary, pre-literacy.
- **Bilingual exposure**: live human input from infancy → bilingualism.
- **Free play**: executive function, social-emotional development.
- **Outdoor / unstructured play**: physical health, attention, mood.

## What the evidence does NOT support

- **Baby-sign**: small-effect-size, often disappears by age 2; limited downstream benefit.
- **Baby-Einstein-style "educational" videos**: no benefit, possible language deficit.
- **Pre-school academic acceleration** (worksheets, flashcards): no evidence of long-term advantage; some evidence of motivational cost.
- **Mandarin / Spanish "exposure" via app**: phoneme discrimination requires live human interaction.
