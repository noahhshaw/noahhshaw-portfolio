# Elementary School Preparation

## Why this matters in year 1

For most NYC and SF Bay Area families considering private elementary (K–5 or K–8), the kindergarten admissions process happens during the year child turns 4. For a 2026-05-11 baby, kindergarten admissions = fall 2030 / spring 2031. This is far in the future, but two things matter now:

1. **Preschool feeders matter.** Some private K–8 schools draw heavily from specific preschools. The 2s/3s/4s preschool you pick can affect K admissions.
2. **Public school strategy depends on geography.** Address-based zoning (NYC, most of US) vs. choice-based (SFUSD lottery) determines whether you need to move now to enable a public-school path.

## NYC — private K–8 / K–12 admissions

### Common timeline (kindergarten entry)

- **September of year child turns 4**: tours and open houses.
- **November of same year**: applications due.
- **January of admission year**: ERB testing (the WPPSI-IV most common for kindergarten admissions; administered by approved psychologists).
- **February**: notification.
- **March**: reply by.

### Top private K–12 / K–8 schools (sample)

Independent Schools Admissions Association of Greater New York directory has ~50 schools. Highly competitive bracket includes:

- **Single-sex girls**: Spence, Brearley, Chapin, Nightingale-Bamford, Hewitt, Marymount, Sacred Heart.
- **Single-sex boys**: Allen-Stevenson, Buckley, St. Bernard's, Collegiate, Trinity (lower school), Browning, Saint David's.
- **Coed**: Dalton, Trevor Day, Horace Mann (Bronx), Riverdale Country, Fieldston, Trinity (upper school is coed; lower school boys), Birch Wathen Lenox, Friends Seminary.
- **Progressive / Reggio**: Bank Street, Ethical Culture Fieldston, Manhattan Country School.

### Preschool → kindergarten feeders

Some preschools have strong placement track records into specific K schools. Examples (from public Parents League data and Independent Schools data):
- 92Y Nursery → wide range of single-sex and coed schools.
- Brick Church → frequent placement at Spence, Nightingale, Buckley.
- Bank Street School (lower school) → not a feeder; their own K.
- Park Avenue Christian Day School → wide range.

### Public option

NYC public elementary is zoned by address. Top zoned schools (PS 6 UES, PS 87 UWS, PS 89 Battery Park, PS 290 Manhattan New School, PS 234 TriBeCa) are free + high-quality but requires living in zone (housing premium reflects this).

NYC G&T (Gifted and Talented) program: separate test in pre-K year for K entry; competitive.

## San Francisco Bay Area — private K–8 / K–12 admissions

### Common timeline (kindergarten entry)

- **September–November of year child turns 4**: open houses, tours.
- **December–January**: applications due (varies by school).
- **March 10–16**: notification.
- **March 16–20**: reply.

### Top private K–8 / K–12 (sample)

SF/Marin/Peninsula:
- Hamlin (girls), Town (boys), Cathedral.
- The San Francisco School, Live Oak, Synergy, Convent of the Sacred Heart, Stuart Hall, KAIS (Korean immersion), CAIS (Chinese immersion), French American International School.
- Marin Country Day, Marin Horizon, Mark Day, Marin Primary.
- Peninsula: Phillips Brooks, Woodland, Castilleja (girls), Crystal Springs Uplands, Nueva, Menlo, Sacred Heart Schools.

East Bay:
- Park Day, Aurora, Tehiyah, Head-Royce, College Prep (UC Prep), Bentley, Black Pine Circle.

### SFUSD lottery system

San Francisco public elementary uses a lottery — no zone-based assignment. Key features:
- **All-residents-eligible** for any school in district.
- **Tiebreakers**: sibling priority, English-Learner status, Census-tract priority for under-served areas.
- **Application opens**: late fall of year child turns 4.
- **Lottery-based** — even for top schools (Alvarado, Clarendon, Rooftop, Lilienthal Mandarin Immersion, etc.).

This means: in SF, you don't move to a "good school district" — you participate in the lottery. Many families combine SFUSD applications with private-school applications as backup.

## What to actually do in year 1

### Months 6–12

- **Identify long list** of K–8 schools to consider (tour as 4-year-old in 2030).
- **Identify preschools** that feed into your target K–8 (your year-2 application).
- **Understand public-school landscape** in your address (NYC zone, SFUSD lottery, suburban district).
- **If considering moving for school zoning**: begin housing search.

### Year 2–3 (for context)

- 2s/3s/4s preschool process active.
- Kindergarten admissions process becomes active in fall of year-child-turns-4.

## Cost

- **Private elementary tuition (NYC)**: $50,000–70,000/year 2026 dollars. Inflated to $65,000–95,000 at year of entry.
- **Private elementary tuition (Bay Area)**: $35,000–55,000/year 2026.
- **K–12 total cost** (if private throughout): $700,000–1.5M+ per child in inflation-adjusted dollars.
- **Financial aid** is broad; many schools have 20–40% of students on aid.

## Strategic considerations

### Stay public, save for college

Public school + heavy 529 + activity-pipeline supplementation. The total household cost difference vs private K–12 can fund full graduate school + a starter home. Many families of considerable means choose this path.

### Public elementary, private high school

PS-zoned NYC + private high school admission (year 8 process). Spence/Brearley/Trinity all have substantial 9th-grade entry.

### Private throughout

The "pipeline" model. Often justified by parents who attended private themselves, value-add of community, network, alumni connections.

### Hybrid — private elementary, public high school (Bay Area in particular)

Less common nationally but works in SF Bay where some public high schools (Lowell, Mission, GW, Burton) are highly competitive.

## What NOT to do

- **Don't make K–12 plans rigidly in year 1.** Family circumstances, child's needs, financial circumstances all shift.
- **Don't rely on "if we just send him to the right preschool, the rest is handled."** Preschool fit doesn't guarantee K placement.
- **Don't underestimate public schools.** In some neighborhoods (UWS, UES NYC, parts of SF, suburban Westchester, Marin) public schools are competitive with private.
