# Breastfeeding vs Formula vs Combo

## Frame the decision honestly

The American debate over breastfeeding is a place where ideology has displaced evidence. The actual evidence base, as Emily Oster summarizes most cleanly in *Cribsheet*[1], is narrower than the popular consensus.

**Robust effects of breastfeeding** (in observational and the few randomized/quasi-randomized studies):
- **Reduced infant gastrointestinal infections** in the first year — moderate effect size, well-replicated.
- **Reduced eczema and possibly some respiratory infections** in early life.
- **Reduced maternal breast cancer risk** — modest, dose-dependent.

**Effects often claimed but NOT well-supported**:
- Higher IQ — confounded by SES; the largest randomized-style study (PROBIT in Belarus) shows a small effect that disappears in better-controlled analyses.
- Lower obesity — confounded; effect disappears with sibling-level controls.
- Stronger emotional bonding — bottle-feeding parents do this just fine; mechanism is co-regulation, not the milk.

**What this means**: breastfeeding has real benefits. Formula has real benefits (consistent supply, partner involvement, maternal mental health, return to work). The benefits of breastfeeding are not so large that formula-feeding is a meaningfully worse outcome for the child.

## AAP recommendation

AAP recommends exclusive breastfeeding for ~6 months, with continued breastfeeding alongside complementary foods for at least 1 year and as long as mutually desired by mother and infant[2]. The 2022 update extended the support to 2 years. This is a population-health recommendation; it is not a moral edict.

## Logistics — breastfeeding

- **Lactation consultant within 72h of discharge.** The first 3 days are the highest-failure-rate window.
- **Weight check at first-week visit** confirms transfer is effective.
- **Output**: ≥6 wet diapers/day, ≥3 stools/day by end of week 1.
- **Feeds 8–12 per 24h** in the newborn period.
- **Vitamin D 400 IU/day supplement** for exclusively/predominantly breastfed infants (formula has it added).
- **Let-down, plugged ducts, mastitis** are real risks. See `mom/breastfeeding-logistics.md`.

## Logistics — formula

- **Choice of formula**: standard cow's-milk-based (Enfamil, Similac, Bobbie, Kirkland) covers >95% of infants. Hypoallergenic (Nutramigen, Alimentum) only if pediatrician confirms milk-protein intolerance; soy formula is rarely first-line.
- **Preparation**: powdered formula prepared per manufacturer instructions; ready-to-feed for first weeks (sterile) is recommended for under-2-month or immunocompromised infants.
- **Bottle**: typical newborn 0–4 mo: 2–4 oz every 3–4 hours; 4–6 mo: 4–6 oz every 3–4 hours; 6–9 mo: 6–8 oz with solids; 9–12 mo: 24–32 oz/day total.
- **Storage**: prepared formula 1h at room temp, 24h refrigerated.
- **No "preheat in microwave"** — uneven heating produces hot pockets.

## Logistics — combo

Combination feeding (some breast, some formula or pumped milk) is operationally common and not problematic. Considerations:

- **Supply**: every direct nursing replaced with a formula/pumped feed reduces supply signaling. If you want to maintain supply, pump when the baby gets a bottle.
- **Bottle preference**: not real for most infants in the first 6 weeks; can become real after.
- **Nipple confusion**: largely myth; infants who can latch can switch between breast and bottle in most cases.

## Decision frame

If breastfeeding is going well and you want to continue: continue. If it's not going well after 4–6 weeks of effort with a lactation consultant: combo or formula is a fully acceptable choice. The data do not support the inference that you're harming the child by formula-feeding.

## Watch-fors

- `[low concern]` Spit-up after feeds; cluster feeding evenings; varying intake day-to-day.
- `[monitor]` Inadequate weight gain; <6 wet diapers/day after first week; persistent fussiness during/after feeds.
- `[call within 24h]` Forceful projectile vomiting (rule out pyloric stenosis); blood in stool (rule out CMPI).
- `[call now]` Severe lethargy + poor feeding; refusal to feed >12h with reduced output.
