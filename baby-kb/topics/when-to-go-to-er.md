# When to Go to the ER vs Urgent Care vs Pediatrician

## Decision frame

| Severity | Where | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Life-threatening, time-critical | 911 / ER | Severe respiratory distress, anaphylaxis, seizure >5 min, unresponsive, severe bleeding, button battery ingestion, suspected meningitis |
| Acute but stable | ER | Fever ≥100.4°F under 90 days, persistent vomiting + lethargy, head injury with concerning features, broken bone, severe burn |
| Sub-acute | Urgent care | Simple lacerations needing stitches, simple ear pain after hours, mild dehydration |
| Routine | Pediatrician | Most fevers >90 days, cold, mild rash, ear pulling, ongoing concerns |
| Triage / question | Pediatrician portal or after-hours line | "Should I worry?" "Is this normal?" |

## ER — go now (or call 911)

### Respiratory

- **Severe distress**: nasal flaring, retractions (skin pulling in around ribs/sternum/clavicles), grunting with each breath, RR >70/min at rest.
- **Cyanosis**: blue/dusky lips, tongue, fingernails. Persistent (not just briefly during crying).
- **Apnea**: pause >20 seconds OR pause with color change.
- **Stridor at rest** (high-pitched inspiratory noise, not just with crying).

### Neurologic

- **Seizure**: any first seizure; any seizure >5 minutes; cluster of seizures.
- **Unresponsive / extreme lethargy**: cannot wake the baby; baby doesn't track or respond.
- **Bulging fontanelle** + fever (suspect meningitis).
- **Loss of acquired motor skills**.
- **Suspected meningitis**: fever + neck stiffness, fever + bulging fontanelle, fever + petechial rash (purple spots that don't blanch under pressure).

### Cardiovascular

- **Severe pallor + lethargy**.
- **Persistent tachycardia** beyond brief crying episodes.
- **Suspected dehydration**: no wet diaper >12h, sunken fontanelle, no tears when crying, lethargy.

### Allergic / immunologic

- **Anaphylaxis**: facial swelling + breathing difficulty + hives + vomiting + lethargy. Within 30 min of food/medication. Call 911.
- **Severe hives + airway involvement** (any throat swelling, stridor, wheezing).

### Trauma

- **Head injury** with: loss of consciousness, persistent vomiting (3+ episodes), unequal pupils, severe headache (in older infant/toddler), seizure, fluid from ears/nose, large soft-spot bulge.
- **Suspected fracture** with deformity.
- **Severe bleeding** that doesn't stop with pressure.
- **Eye injury** with blood inside the eye, visible scratch, persistent crying after eye contact with object.
- **Burn** large or to face/genitals.
- **Near-drowning**: even if "fine afterward" — delayed pulmonary edema risk; ER same-day.

### Toxic ingestion

- **Button battery** suspected: ER immediately, do not wait for symptoms.
- **Magnets** (multiple) suspected.
- **Caustic chemical** ingestion (drain cleaner, oven cleaner, laundry pod) — Poison Control 1-800-222-1222 first; many resolve with phone advice; emergent if symptoms.
- **Medication overdose** (any unknown amount of any medication).
- **Plant ingestion** — Poison Control first.

### Fever-specific

- **Fever ≥100.4°F rectal in infant <90 days** — ER same-day. Full sepsis workup standard.
- **Fever + petechial rash** (rule out meningococcemia).
- **Fever + lethargy disproportionate** at any age.

### Other

- **Persistent inconsolable crying** with abdominal distension and no stooling — rule out volvulus/intussusception.
- **Bloody stools** (not streaks from constipation; actual blood).
- **Bilious (green) vomiting** in infant — surgical concern.
- **Forceful projectile vomiting** + dehydration (rule out pyloric stenosis).

## Urgent care — go today

- Lacerations needing stitches but bleeding controlled.
- Simple suspected fractures without deformity.
- Mild dehydration with stable vitals.
- Severe ear pain after hours when pediatrician's office is closed.
- Foreign body in nose/ear (non-button-battery).
- Severe rash without systemic features.

## Pediatrician — same-day or 24h

- Fever ≥100.4°F under 6 mo (call same-day, may direct to ER).
- Persistent fever >102°F at any age beyond newborn.
- Persistent cough, congestion >7 days.
- Ear-pulling + fever + irritability.
- Rash that's spreading or appears infectious (chickenpox-like, hand-foot-mouth-like).
- New onset wheezing or stridor.
- Eye discharge or redness.
- Refusing feeds for >12h.

## Pediatrician — non-urgent (portal/scheduled)

- Mild rashes resolving.
- Cradle cap.
- Constipation.
- Spitting up (well-baby pattern).
- Sleep questions.
- Feeding-routine questions.
- Developmental questions.

## Setup before you need it

- **Pediatrician on speed dial** including after-hours triage line.
- **Poison Control**: 1-800-222-1222 (US national).
- **Nearest pediatric ER** identified in advance, route memorized. Adult ERs can manage pediatric emergencies but pediatric ERs are equipped for infant-specific care.
- **Insurance card photo** in your phone.
- **Health summary** (allergies, medications, recent vaccines, growth percentiles) in a note for ER context.

## Watch-fors meta-rule

When in doubt, call. The pediatrician's after-hours triage nurse can help sort. The downside of an unneeded call is small; the downside of a missed serious illness is large.
