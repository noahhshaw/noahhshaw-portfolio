# Nanny vs Daycare vs Au Pair

## Decision frame

Three primary options for full-time childcare in year 1: nanny (in-home), daycare (out-of-home center), au pair (live-in). Each has different cost structure, developmental implications, and operational realities.

## Cost (rough 2026 US, NYC/SF benchmarks)

| Option | Annual cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Nanny | $65,000–110,000+ | Higher in NYC/SF. Add taxes, payroll service, benefits. |
| Daycare (full-time, infant room) | $24,000–48,000 | NYC/SF top-tier $36,000–60,000. |
| Au pair | $20,000–28,000 | Plus room/board, car often required. Cultural exchange visa program. |
| Nanny share (split with another family) | $35,000–55,000 each | Halves nanny cost; doubles supervision. |

## Developmental considerations

### What the research actually shows

The NICHD Study of Early Child Care and Youth Development tracked >1,300 children from infancy to age 15[1]. Key findings:

- **Quality matters more than type.** High-quality daycare and high-quality nanny care produce comparable cognitive and social outcomes.
- **Quantity matters slightly.** More than 30 hr/week in non-parental care correlates with modest increases in externalizing behavior at age 4.5 (small effect; disappears by adolescence in most studies).
- **Daycare confers slight cognitive boost** — language, school readiness — likely due to peer interaction and structured curriculum. Effect size small.
- **Daycare confers more illness exposure** — infants in daycare get 8–12 illnesses in year 1 vs. ~4–6 in home care. Long-term: net immunological benefit; some evidence of reduced asthma in adolescence.
- **Stable primary caregivers + secure attachment** is the dominant predictor regardless of type.

### Operational implications

**Daycare** — pros:
- Predictable, redundant (no "nanny called in sick" crisis).
- Built-in peer interaction.
- Structured curriculum.
- Lower per-hour cost.
- Pre-K pipeline (some daycares feed into preschools).

**Daycare** — cons:
- Inflexible hours (typical 7am–6pm with hard cutoffs).
- Closed for holidays, illness outbreaks, weather.
- Can't accept sick infants (any fever above thresholds).
- 1:3 to 1:4 staff ratios in infant rooms (varies by state).
- Frequent illness in first 6 months of attendance.

**Nanny** — pros:
- 1:1 attention.
- Flexible hours (within your contract).
- Sick care possible.
- Schedule continuity (no closures).
- Customized routine.

**Nanny** — cons:
- Single point of failure (nanny illness, vacation, departure).
- Higher cost.
- Employer responsibilities (W-2, payroll taxes, workers' comp depending on state).
- Less peer interaction.
- Quality variance — vetting depends on you.

**Au pair** — pros:
- Lowest cost full-time live-in option.
- Cultural exposure (often a second language).
- Some flexibility.

**Au pair** — cons:
- 12-month visa cycle — turnover annually.
- Live-in privacy implications.
- Lower experience level (most au pairs 18–26, may have no childcare experience).
- Limited to 45 hr/week with 1.5 days off; cannot drop below sleep hours.

## Hybrid models

- **Nanny share**: split a nanny with another family. Halves cost; provides peer interaction; requires alignment between families on schedule, philosophy, illness policy.
- **Daycare + occasional nanny**: daycare 4 days, nanny 1 day; or daycare 8am–4pm + nanny 4pm–6pm covering parent commute time.
- **Family support**: grandparents covering 1–2 days/week reduces costs and builds bond.

## Quality indicators (to look for in any setting)

- **Caregiver-to-infant ratio**: 1:3 (daycare infant room) or 1:1 (nanny). Lower is better but cost-driven.
- **Caregiver stability**: low turnover. Ask about staff tenure.
- **Trained / experienced**: CPR + first-aid certification, infant care experience.
- **Responsive caregiving**: in observation, do they pick up baby promptly when crying? Do they speak to infants directly (vs. just managing)?
- **Safe sleep practices**: babies on backs, cribs only, no loose bedding.
- **Clean, well-organized environment**.
- **Communication**: daily updates (Brightwheel, Tadpoles, daily form, daily text).
- **Curriculum / activities** appropriate to age: for infants, this is mostly floor time + reading + outdoor time, not "academic" content.

## Daycare illness — realistic expectations

See `topics/daycare-illness-realities.md`. Summary: 8–12 illnesses in year 1 of daycare attendance is normal. Most are URIs and brief GI illnesses. Plan for 1 day/week sick during the first 2 months of attendance.

## Operational setup

### Nanny

- **Vetting**: agency (Hello Sitter, Tend, Adventure Nannies — vary by city) provides background checks and references; direct hire (Care.com, Sittercity) requires you to do checks yourself.
- **Background check**: minimum criminal records, motor vehicle records, references. Some agencies include drug testing.
- **Trial week** before full hire.
- **Contract**: hours, pay, benefits, holidays, sick days, taxes, termination terms.
- **Payroll**: HomePay (Care.com) or HomeWork Solutions handle nanny payroll/taxes.
- **Workers' comp**: required in some states (NY, CA among them).
- **Daily routine document**: written for consistency.

### Daycare

- **Tour 5+ centers** before selecting.
- **Waitlists**: top NYC/SF infant centers have 6–18 month waitlists. Apply during pregnancy.
- **Contract**: usually requires 1–3 month notice to leave; deposits typical.
- **Vaccines current** for caregivers (Tdap, flu, MMR).
- **Sick policy** in writing — what fever sends baby home, return-to-care criteria.

### Au pair

- **Through agency** (Cultural Care, AuPairCare, etc.) — visa-sponsored J-1 program.
- **Match interview**: video calls, references, fit assessment.
- **House setup**: dedicated room, transportation arrangements, family integration plan.
- **Annual rematch** if needed; second-year extension possible.

## Tax considerations

- **Dependent Care FSA**: $5,000/year pre-tax through employer (use-it-or-lose-it). Covers childcare expenses.
- **Child and Dependent Care Tax Credit**: 20–35% of up to $3,000 in care expenses (one child). Varies by AGI.
- **Nanny payroll taxes**: 7.65% Social Security/Medicare paid by employer + employee; federal/state unemployment taxes; possibly state disability.

## Watch-fors during the transition

- `[low concern]` 2–3 weeks of adjustment-related fussiness.
- `[monitor]` Persistent inability to settle; weight stagnation; persistent illness pattern.
- `[call within 24h]` First fever ≥100.4°F under 90 days.
- `[call now]` Severe respiratory distress, lethargy, dehydration.
