# Common Illnesses — Year One

## RSV (Respiratory Syncytial Virus)

**What**: most common cause of severe lower respiratory infection in infants. Peak season Oct–Mar in temperate US.
**Symptoms**: starts as a cold (runny nose, cough), can progress to bronchiolitis (wheezing, fast/labored breathing, poor feeding) over 2–5 days.
**Risk by age**: peak hospitalization risk 0–6 mo. Premature, immunocompromised, or congenital heart disease infants at higher risk.
**Prevention**: nirsevimab (or maternal RSVpreF). See `topics/vaccines-overview.md`.
**Treatment**: supportive (suction, hydration, oxygen if needed). No specific antiviral.
**Watch-fors**:
- `[monitor]` Cold symptoms in infant.
- `[call within 24h]` Wheezing; respiratory rate >60/min at rest; reduced feeding; cough worsening day 3–5.
- `[call now]` Severe retractions (skin pulling in around ribs/sternum), nasal flaring, blue/dusky color, lethargy disproportionate to fever, RR >70/min.

## Hand-Foot-Mouth Disease (Coxsackievirus)

**What**: viral; rash on hands, feet, mouth, sometimes diaper area. Usually mild.
**Symptoms**: 1–2 days of fever, then characteristic rash (red spots, sometimes blisters); sores in mouth painful and reduce intake.
**Treatment**: supportive (acetaminophen for pain/fever, hydration). Resolves in 7–10 days.
**Contagion**: highly contagious — daycare exposure common in summer/fall.
**Watch-fors**:
- `[low concern]` Classic rash with mild fever, eating/drinking.
- `[monitor]` Painful mouth sores reducing intake.
- `[call within 24h]` Refusing fluids, signs of dehydration (<5 wet diapers/day).
- `[call now]` Severe dehydration, lethargy, neurologic signs (rare encephalitis).

## Croup

**What**: viral inflammation of the larynx/upper trachea (parainfluenza most common). Typical age 6 mo–3 yr. Worse at night.
**Symptoms**: barking cough, hoarse voice, stridor (high-pitched inspiratory noise), worse with crying or at night.
**Treatment**: cool mist (open freezer briefly, cold outside air, steamy bathroom). Pediatrician may prescribe oral steroid (dexamethasone) for moderate cases.
**Watch-fors**:
- `[low concern]` Mild barking cough, no stridor, eating/drinking.
- `[monitor]` Stridor only with crying.
- `[call within 24h]` Stridor at rest improving with mist.
- `[call now]` Stridor at rest persistent, retractions, drooling/inability to swallow (rule out epiglottitis), blue color, severe distress.

## Otitis Media (Ear Infection)

**What**: bacterial or viral infection of the middle ear. Peak incidence 6 mo–24 mo, often follows URI.
**Symptoms**: ear pulling/rubbing, irritability, fever, sleep disruption, sometimes drainage. Frequently bilateral.
**Treatment**: AAP recommends watchful waiting 48–72h for uncomplicated cases >6 mo; antibiotics for severe cases or non-improving. Pain control (acetaminophen, ibuprofen).
**Watch-fors**:
- `[low concern]` Mild ear pulling, no fever.
- `[monitor]` Fever + ear pulling >24h.
- `[call within 24h]` Persistent fever >102°F + ear pulling; ear drainage.
- `[call now]` Severe pain + lethargy; mastoid (bone behind ear) tenderness/redness — rare complication.

## URI (Common Cold)

**What**: rhinovirus, coronavirus, adenovirus, etc. Daycare-attending infants average 8–12 colds in year 1.
**Symptoms**: runny nose, cough, low-grade fever, fussiness. Resolves in 7–10 days.
**Treatment**: supportive — saline nose drops + bulb suction (NoseFrida or similar) for congestion, hydration, humidifier.
**No medications recommended for under-2** — no decongestants, no cough suppressants. AAP discourages over-the-counter cold medicine for under-6.
**Watch-fors**:
- `[low concern]` Classic URI, eating/drinking.
- `[monitor]` Cough lasting >2 weeks.
- `[call within 24h]` Cold features + fever ≥100.4°F at <90 days; cold worsening day 5+.
- `[call now]` Respiratory distress (see RSV signs).

## Influenza

**What**: seasonal flu, typically Nov–Apr.
**Symptoms**: sudden-onset high fever, fatigue, body aches, cough, runny nose. Worse in young infants.
**Prevention**: annual flu vaccine from 6 mo.
**Treatment**: oseltamivir (Tamiflu) within 48h of onset can shorten course; supportive otherwise.
**Watch-fors**:
- `[call within 24h]` Fever ≥100.4°F at <90 days; high fever + lethargy.
- `[call now]` Severe respiratory distress; persistent vomiting + dehydration; seizure.

## COVID-19

**What**: SARS-CoV-2.
**Symptoms in infants**: usually mild — runny nose, cough, low-grade fever. Severe disease less common than in older adults but possible, especially under 6 mo.
**Treatment**: supportive.
**Watch-fors**: same as RSV/flu — respiratory rate, color, feeding, lethargy.

## Gastroenteritis (Viral)

**What**: rotavirus (rare since vaccine), norovirus, adenovirus.
**Symptoms**: vomiting, diarrhea, sometimes fever.
**Treatment**: oral rehydration (Pedialyte preferred over water alone). Continue breast/formula feeds. BRAT diet (banana, rice, applesauce, toast) for older infants is folkloric — current AAP guidance is "regular age-appropriate diet."
**Watch-fors**:
- `[low concern]` 1–2 episodes of vomiting, brief loose stool.
- `[monitor]` Persistent vomiting; reduced output.
- `[call within 24h]` <5 wet diapers/24h; persistent vomiting >12h; blood in stool.
- `[call now]` No wet diaper >12h; lethargy; persistent vomiting + lethargy; bloody stool with severe abdominal pain.

## Roseola

**What**: HHV-6 virus, age 6–24 mo typical.
**Symptoms**: 3–5 days of high fever (102–105°F), then fever drops and rash appears (small pink spots on trunk). Rash is *post-fever*, not concurrent.
**Treatment**: supportive.
**Watch-fors**:
- `[low concern]` Classic pattern with rash appearing as fever resolves.
- `[call within 24h]` Persistent fever >5 days; lethargy.
- `[call now]` Febrile seizure (common with roseola fever spikes); severe lethargy.

## When to ER vs urgent care vs pediatrician

- **ER**: respiratory distress, persistent vomiting + dehydration, seizure, lethargy disproportionate to illness, fever in infant <90 days, severe injury, suspected meningitis (fever + bulging fontanelle/neck stiffness/petechial rash), suspected anaphylaxis.
- **Urgent care**: simple lacerations needing stitching, simple fractures suspected, persistent low-grade illness when pediatrician unavailable.
- **Pediatrician**: most fevers, URIs, mild rashes, ear-pulling, ongoing concerns.
- **Pediatrician via portal/triage line**: routine questions; severity sorting.
