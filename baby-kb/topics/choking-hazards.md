# Choking Hazards

## Why this matters

Choking is a leading cause of injury and death in children under 4. Most fatal choking events are food-related. Most non-fatal events (and many fatal) involve small toys or household objects. Gagging is normal during eating; choking (silent, no air movement) is emergent.

## Foods to avoid until age 4 (AAP)[1]

These shapes/textures are the highest choking-risk forms in young children:

- **Whole grapes, cherries with pits, whole berries** — quarter lengthwise.
- **Hot dogs (whole or cross-section coins)** — quarter lengthwise. Better to avoid until 4.
- **Whole nuts** — never. Nut butter thinned and spread thinly is fine from 6 mo for allergen introduction.
- **Hard or chewy candy, gum** — never under 4.
- **Popcorn** — never under 4.
- **Marshmallows** — never under 4.
- **Raw hard vegetables** (whole carrots, raw celery, raw apple chunks) — cooked or grated.
- **Large meat chunks** — finely shredded or cut.
- **Peanut butter spoonful** (clumping risk) — thin spread on toast, mix into oatmeal/yogurt.
- **Round-shaped cheese chunks** — quarter into thin rectangles.

## Non-food choking hazards

- **Coins** (any denomination).
- **Button batteries** (life-threatening — ER immediately if suspected).
- **Magnets** (especially rare-earth/neodymium — multiple ingested cause bowel perforation).
- **Small toy parts** (anything that fits through a toilet paper tube).
- **Marbles, small balls** (smaller than 1.75 inches diameter).
- **Latex balloons** (uninflated or popped pieces — inhaled to lungs, dangerous).
- **Dryer sheets, plastic wrap, plastic bags** — suffocation more than choking, but related.
- **Pieces of toys with magnets, batteries, or small detached parts.**
- **Pen caps, eraser pieces, pencils** (older sibling/parent items left within reach).
- **Pieces of dog/cat kibble** for dog/cat households.
- **Coins from purse spilled on floor.**

## Choking vs. gagging

| Sign | Gagging (normal) | Choking (emergency) |
|---|---|---|
| Sound | Coughing, sputtering, retching | Silent or high-pitched whistle |
| Color | Pink, may flush red briefly | Pale, blue, dusky |
| Movement | Forward leaning, mouth open, may gag food out | Distress, unable to clear |
| Recovery | Self-resolves in 10–30 sec | Doesn't self-resolve |

Gagging is the body's protective mechanism. Don't intervene unnecessarily — the gag reflex usually clears the obstruction. Intervene if there's no air movement.

## Choking response — infant (under 12 months)

1. **Confirm choking** (no air movement, no sound, distress, color change).
2. **Call 911** (or have someone call while you act).
3. **5 back blows**: face-down on your forearm, head lower than body, heel of hand between shoulder blades.
4. **5 chest thrusts**: turn baby face-up; two fingers on lower half of breastbone; quick, sharp thrusts.
5. **Repeat back blows + chest thrusts** until object dislodges or baby loses consciousness.
6. **If baby loses consciousness**: begin infant CPR — 30 chest compressions (1.5 inches deep) followed by 2 breaths; check mouth for visible object before each breath cycle.

**Do NOT** do abdominal thrusts (Heimlich) on infants under 1 year — risk of internal injury.

## Choking response — toddler (1+ year)

1. Confirm choking.
2. Call 911.
3. **Abdominal thrusts** (Heimlich): stand behind, fist above navel, quick upward thrusts.
4. Repeat until dislodged or unconscious.
5. CPR if unconscious.

## Take a class

The American Red Cross runs infant/child CPR + choking classes weekly in most cities. ~2 hours, ~$60–100. Both parents take it before baby is mobile. AAP and AHA both recommend it.

## Specifically: high-protein foods and the "gagging mid-meal"

When introducing solids (especially BLW), gagging mid-meal is common in the first weeks. Most often the gag reflex clears the food. Stay close, don't intervene unless choking criteria met. Sit-up position is the active safety factor.

## Watch-fors

- `[low concern]` Brief gag with self-clearing.
- `[monitor]` Multiple gagging episodes per meal — consider smaller pieces, softer textures.
- `[call within 24h]` Suspected partial obstruction (persistent cough after meal, refuses food, drooling more than usual — possible foreign body in airway).
- `[call now]` Choking with no air movement; suspected button battery or magnet ingestion; lost consciousness from a choking event.
