# Introducing Solids

## When to start

AAP recommends starting solids around **6 months**, with three readiness signals[1]:
1. Sits with minimal support.
2. Lost the tongue-thrust reflex (food goes back, not out).
3. Shows interest (reaches for food, opens mouth).

Some pediatricians recommend earlier (4 months) for high-risk allergen introduction. The NIAID 2017 Addendum, derived from the LEAP trial, recommends:
- **High-risk infants** (severe eczema or known egg allergy): consult pediatric allergist; introduce peanut at 4–6 mo with allergist-supervised first dose if indicated.
- **Moderate-risk infants** (mild–moderate eczema): introduce peanut around 6 mo at home.
- **Low-risk infants**: introduce peanut around 6 mo with other solids[2].

## Two methods — purée vs baby-led weaning

### Purées (traditional)

Spoon-feed thin then thicker purées; progress to mashed, then chopped textures by 9–12 mo. Parent controls intake.

**Pros**: easier to verify intake; less mess; easier in restaurants.
**Cons**: more parent time; potential delay in self-feeding skill; some infants resist textures introduced later.

### Baby-Led Weaning (BLW)

Skip purées; offer soft, appropriately sized finger foods baby self-feeds from start. Examples: avocado strips, well-cooked sweet potato wedges, banana spears, soft scrambled egg, well-cooked pasta.

**Pros**: self-paced intake; better fine-motor practice; family-meal continuity; possibly broader long-term taste acceptance.
**Cons**: massive mess; perceived choking concern (data does not support a real-risk increase — more on this below); harder to verify intake.

### Choking risk: BLW vs purées

Concerns that BLW increases choking risk are not supported by the available evidence. The BLISS study (NZ randomized trial, 2016) found no difference in choking incidence between BLW and traditional spoon-feeding[3]. AAP-cited reviews concur. The key safety rules apply equally to both methods:
- No choking-hazard shapes/sizes (whole grapes, hot dogs, popcorn, whole nuts, hard raw vegetables).
- Always supervised, infant in upright position.
- Never propped or feeding while reclined.

## Allergen introduction protocol

Top 9 allergens (FALCPA-defined): milk, eggs, peanut, tree nuts, soy, wheat, fish, shellfish, sesame.

**Protocol** (synthesized from NIAID Addendum and AAP):
1. Start with low-allergen foods (avocado, sweet potato, banana, oatmeal cereal) for 2–3 days to confirm general food tolerance.
2. Introduce one allergen at a time, ideally in the morning, on a day you'll be home for 2 hours.
3. **Peanut**: 1 tsp smooth peanut butter thinned in 1 tbsp warm water, OR 2 tsp Bamba/peanut puffs softened in milk. Whole peanuts/tree nuts forbidden until age 4 (choking).
4. **Egg**: well-cooked, including yolk. Start with ¼ tsp; build up.
5. **Cow's milk**: in form of yogurt/cheese starting around 6 mo. Whole cow's milk as a drink waits until 12 mo (low iron, kidney load).
6. **Wheat**: cereal, pasta, bread.
7. **Soy**: tofu, edamame mashed.
8. **Fish/shellfish**: finely shredded cooked salmon, etc.
9. **Sesame**: tahini thinned in food, sesame seeds blended.

**Maintain exposure 2–3× weekly** for sustained tolerance — single-shot introduction does not maintain tolerance per LEAP follow-up data.

## Salt, sugar, honey

- **No honey before 12 months** — botulism risk. Hard rule.
- **No added salt before 12 months** — kidney load.
- **No added sugar before 24 months** per AAP/Dietary Guidelines.
- **No cow's milk as primary drink before 12 months** (yogurt and cheese are fine starting 6 mo).

## First foods — practical menu

Week 1: avocado, banana, sweet potato (each, separately, 2–3 days each).
Week 2: oatmeal cereal (iron-fortified), egg yolk well-cooked.
Week 3: peanut (introduce at home, AM, 2h observation).
Week 4–6: expand — yogurt, soft cheese, salmon, lentils.
Months 7–9: variety of textures (mashed → chopped → soft finger foods).
Months 9–12: mostly table foods, family meals, sized appropriately.

## Iron

Iron stores deplete around 6 mo for breastfed infants. Iron-rich first foods:
- Iron-fortified infant cereal (oatmeal, multigrain).
- Pureed/finely chopped beef, chicken, lentils.
- Spinach, kale (well-cooked).
Pair iron-rich foods with vitamin C foods (citrus, peppers, tomatoes) to enhance absorption.

## Watch-fors

- `[low concern]` Mild rashes around mouth (irritant from acidic foods); food refusal of new foods (5–10 exposures average for acceptance).
- `[monitor]` Persistent rash post-food; mucus in stool; persistent diarrhea.
- `[call within 24h]` Hives beyond contact area; vomiting within 2h; lethargy after a food.
- `[call now]` Anaphylaxis: facial swelling, breathing difficulty, hives + vomiting + lethargy. Call 911.
