# Weaning from Breast or Bottle

## Why bottle-weaning by 12–18 months

AAP recommends transitioning off the bottle by 12–18 months. Evidence base: prolonged bottle use is associated with iron deficiency (excess milk crowds out iron-rich foods), dental caries (especially with sleep-time bottles), and delayed oral-motor skills[1]. Sippy/straw cups are bridge tools; the open cup is the developmental target.

## Bottle weaning sequence

Suggested 2–4 week protocol:

1. **Reduce daytime bottles first.** Replace 1 daytime bottle per week with a meal + cup (water with food, milk in cup at meal).
2. **Mid-morning and mid-afternoon bottles dropped first** (these are usually the easiest to skip — meal + snack covers the calorie need).
3. **Pre-nap and pre-bed bottles last.** Replace pre-nap with a small cup of milk consumed at the table, then nap routine.
4. **Pre-bed bottle**: separate the milk-bottle from the sleep environment by 2–3 weeks. Move bottle to before bath, not after. Then transition to milk in a cup before bath.

## Breastfeeding weaning

If mother has been nursing and chooses to stop entirely (some continue to 18+ mo or 2+ yr per AAP):

### Gradual weaning (recommended)

- Drop one feed per week. Start with the feed least associated with comfort (often a daytime feed, after solids well-established).
- Substitute with cup or bottle of formula (under 12 mo) or whole milk (12+ mo).
- Last feeds to drop: typically the morning and bedtime feeds, in that order.
- Total timeline: 4–8 weeks for full weaning.

### Mother's body during weaning

- Engorgement is the main risk. Pump for relief only — do not pump fully (signals supply to maintain).
- Plugged ducts and mastitis risk elevated during weaning. See `mom/breastfeeding-logistics.md`.
- Hormonal shifts (drop in prolactin/oxytocin) can cause mood dip in some women — anticipate and watch for low mood, especially if PPD history.
- Sage tea, sudafed (decongestants), and cabbage leaves are folk remedies with weak evidence; mainly comfort-based.

## Common questions

**"She refuses the bottle and I'm returning to work."** Try: someone other than mother offering it; different bottles/nipples (Comotomo, Dr. Brown's, Phillips Avent — Wirecutter Crackers); body-warmed milk; offering when not very hungry; mother out of the house during the trial.

**"She's 14 mo and won't drink milk in a cup."** Try: open cup with help; straw cup (Munchkin Miracle 360, Honey Bear); diluted milk in fun cup; smoothies with hidden milk; yogurt and cheese as alternative dairy. Keep offering — acceptance often takes 10+ exposures.

**"Should we use a sippy cup?"** Speech-language pathologists and AAP increasingly favor open cups and straw cups over hard-spout sippy cups, which mimic the bottle and may delay oral-motor development. Sippy is a tolerable bridge but not the long-term target.

## Watch-fors

- `[low concern]` 2–3 days of resistance during transition.
- `[monitor]` Persistent refusal of all milk substitutes; weight stagnation.
- `[call within 24h]` Persistent reduced intake → reduced output.
- `[call now]` Severe maternal mastitis (fever + breast redness + flu-like symptoms).
