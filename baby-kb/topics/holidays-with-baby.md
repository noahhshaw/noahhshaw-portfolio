# Holidays with Baby

## Frame

The first year of holidays is largely meaningful for the parents and grandparents. The baby experiences holiday gatherings as: more strangers than usual, schedule disruption, excessive light/noise/handing-around. Plan the day to protect routines; the meaning lives in your photos.

## General principles

1. **Protect naps and bedtime.** The 4pm nap blowout creates a 5pm meltdown that destroys evening events. Schedule around her sleep, not the other way around.
2. **Limit handing-around** to people she knows + warm-up time for new faces. Stranger anxiety peaks 7–10 mo and is full-strength through holidays for that age cohort.
3. **One major event per day.** Drive-to-grandparents + dinner + church + photos in one day collapses for everyone.
4. **Pre-vaccinate adult contacts.** Tdap booster, flu, COVID-19 current. Pertussis from adult contacts is the single most-likely-to-kill-an-infant vaccine-preventable infection.
5. **Travel days are not holiday days.** Build in a recovery day on either end.

## Per-holiday playbook (US-centric calendar)

### Halloween (~Day 173, late October)

Baby is ~6 mo. Costume optional; trick-or-treating not relevant for this age. Photo opportunity. Decorations: avoid choking-hazard small items (mini pumpkins, candy corn, plastic spider rings).

### Thanksgiving (~Day 197, late November)

Baby is ~6.5 mo. Solids established by now; soft Thanksgiving foods are appropriate (mashed sweet potato, well-cooked turkey shredded, mashed cranberry sauce no added sugar). Avoid: pecan/walnut pieces, hard turkey skin.

Hosting tradeoffs: hosting allows control of routine; traveling requires sleep-environment recreation.

### December holidays (Christmas / Hanukkah / Kwanzaa, ~Day 225–245)

Baby is ~7.5–8 mo. Usually peak stranger-anxiety window. Family travel intensifies; photo opportunities maximize.

- **Tree safety**: anchor tree, low-hung ornaments shatter-proof, no tinsel (ingestion + magnet hazard with old foil), no lit candles within reach (Hanukkah candles in baby-inaccessible window).
- **Gift-opening**: ribbons, small décor, batteries from new toys are choking/ingestion hazards.
- **Big meals**: same solids guidance as Thanksgiving.
- **Hosting expectations**: lower them. "We're here, we'd love to see you, baby naps at X and we leave by Y."

### New Year's

Baby is ~7.5 mo. She's asleep at midnight. The "first New Year's" is a parental moment.

### Valentine's Day (~Day 279, mid-February)

Baby is ~9 mo. Photo prop holiday.

### Easter / Passover (March–April)

Baby is ~10–11 mo. Easter egg hunts not yet relevant; photo opportunities.

### Mother's Day / Father's Day

May / June — Mother's Day around her first birthday window for a 2026-05-11 baby. Synchronize with birthday plans.

### Cultural-specific holidays

The same logic applies: protect routine, limit overstimulation, vaccinate adult contacts. Specific traditions (mundan/akkika/baby naming, baekil, dol, christening, brit milah) require pre-planning with religious officiants and family travelers.

## Travel during holidays

See `topics/travel-with-infant.md` for general travel logistics. Holiday-specific:

- **Booking**: 2–4 months out for peak demand.
- **Flights**: bulkhead seat where possible (bassinet for under-6-mo on long-haul); aisle seat pre-bulkhead for short-haul.
- **Documents**: passport for international travel — 6–12 weeks for first-time infant passport, both parents present.
- **Sleep environment portable**: pack-n-play, blackout sheet, white noise machine. The Slumberpod is popular for hotel rooms with parents.

## Photos and traditions

This is the year you'll set photo traditions: matching pajamas Christmas morning, pumpkin patch in October, family dinner table portrait. Tradition-creation matters more than execution polish.

## Watch-fors

- `[low concern]` Sleep disruption around travel/visits; brief illness post-extended-family contact.
- `[monitor]` URI cluster from holiday family gatherings.
- `[call within 24h]` First post-holiday fever ≥100.4°F under 90 days; persistent illness.
- `[call now]` Severe respiratory distress; choking on holiday food; allergic reaction.
