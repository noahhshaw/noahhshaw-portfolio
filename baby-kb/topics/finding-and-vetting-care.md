# Finding and Vetting Childcare

## Finding

### Nanny — sources, ranked by reliability

1. **Specialized agency** (e.g., Hello Sitter [SF], Adventure Nannies, Tend [NYC]) — high cost (placement fees often $5,000–15,000), pre-screened, references checked, replacement guarantee. Best for first-time hire.
2. **General agency** — Care.com, Sittercity, UrbanSitter — moderate cost, you do screening.
3. **Friends-of-friends referral** — cheapest, often best fit; limited supply.
4. **Local parent groups / Slack channels** — Bay Area / NYC have active parent groups.
5. **Nanny-specific Slack/community** (varies by city).

### Daycare — sources

1. **Visit centers in person.** Tour at least 5 in your geography.
2. **State licensing database** — confirms licensed, lists violations (CA: ccld.dss.ca.gov; NY: ocfs.ny.gov).
3. **Wirecutter / parents-network reviews.**
4. **Top employer benefits** — some firms have backup or full-care benefits (Bright Horizons, KinderCare partnerships).

### Au pair — agencies

- **Cultural Care Au Pair** (largest US program).
- **AuPairCare**.
- **GoAuPair**.
- **Interexchange**.

All US au pair programs operate under Department of State J-1 visa rules — annual cycles, defined wage minimums.

## Vetting — nanny specifically

### Documents

- **Photo ID + Social Security card**.
- **I-9 work authorization**.
- **Driver's license + auto insurance** (if driving baby).
- **CPR + infant first-aid certification**.
- **Background check authorization** (signed).

### Background checks (minimum)

- **Criminal records** — federal + state. Multiple-state if she's lived elsewhere.
- **Sex offender registry**.
- **Motor vehicle records** (if driving).
- **Reference check** — minimum 3 prior employers, contacted directly.
- **Optional but recommended**: credit check (in some states only with consent), drug screen.

### Reference questions to ask

- "How long did [nanny] work for you?"
- "What were her primary responsibilities?"
- "How was she with [age range]?"
- "How did she handle [crisis: illness, accident, parent emergency]?"
- "Why did the relationship end?"
- "Would you hire her again? On a 1–10 scale, how strongly?"
- "Anything I should know that I haven't asked?"

The "anything I haven't asked" question is the highest-yield. Most candor comes after the structured questions.

### Trial week

A paid 3–5 day trial before full hire. Observe directly when possible. Cameras (with disclosure to nanny) are common in NY and CA.

### Contract — what to include

- Hours and overtime rate.
- Pay rate (gross and net), pay schedule, payment method.
- Federal/state taxes withheld (you're the employer).
- Health insurance contribution if applicable.
- Holidays paid (typical: 6 federal holidays + nanny's birthday).
- Vacation (typical: 2 weeks/year, 1 of nanny's choice + 1 of family's choice).
- Sick days (typical: 5–10/year).
- Mileage reimbursement if she uses her car (current IRS rate ~67¢/mile in 2026).
- Confidentiality terms.
- Termination notice (typical: 2 weeks each direction).
- Exit bonus (some families build in 1–2 weeks at termination as a stability incentive).

### Pay and taxes

- **Cash under-the-table is illegal** and creates risk on both sides (no Social Security credits for nanny; potential audit/penalty for family).
- **Hourly wage** in NYC/SF: $25–40/hr typical for experienced nanny.
- **Overtime**: per FLSA, 1.5× after 40 hours.
- **Payroll service**: HomePay (Care.com), HomeWork Solutions, Poppins Payroll. Costs ~$60–80/month; handles withholdings, W-2 issuance, state filings.

### Workers' comp

Required in NY, CA, NJ, and many other states. ~$300–600/year through state-specific programs.

## Vetting — daycare specifically

### Tour checklist

- **Caregiver-to-infant ratio** in the room.
- **Number of caregivers per shift** (consistency matters).
- **Curriculum**: ask to see the daily/weekly schedule.
- **Sick policy**: what symptoms send baby home.
- **Vaccine policy** for staff and other families.
- **Communication**: app, daily reports, photo updates.
- **Parent access**: are parents welcome to drop in unannounced?
- **Outdoor time**: how much, where, what weather thresholds.
- **Sleep practices**: ABC (alone, back, crib), no bumpers, no swaddles after rolling.
- **Food**: provided or BYO; allergens policy; allergen training.
- **Clean / safe**: visible playgrounds, padded floors, baby-proofed kitchen access.
- **Turnover**: how long has the head caregiver been here? The room caregiver?
- **Licensing** — current and clean.
- **Hours, holidays, closures** — annual calendar.
- **Cost structure**: tuition, deposit, sibling discount.

### Red flags

- Reluctance to share contact information for current parents.
- Caregivers on phones during observation.
- TVs on during infant care.
- Cribs with bumpers, loose bedding.
- High turnover patterns.
- Defensive responses to safety questions.

## Watch-fors during transition

- `[low concern]` 1–3 weeks of adjustment fussiness.
- `[monitor]` Persistent crying through entire day; weight stagnation; behavioral change at home.
- `[call within 24h]` Suspected mistreatment (unexplained marks, fearful response to specific caregiver).
- `[call now]` Severe injury at care; suspected abuse — call CPS in your state + pediatrician.
