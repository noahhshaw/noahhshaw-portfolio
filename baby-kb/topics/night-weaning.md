# Night Weaning

## When to do it

Most babies become physiologically capable of sleeping through the night without feeds between 4 and 6 months, depending on weight gain trajectory and pediatrician sign-off[1]. The capability does not equal the habit — many infants continue to wake from learned association even after they don't need the calories.

Decision points:
- **Pediatrician confirms growth on track.** Weight at or above the 10th percentile typically clears the medical bar.
- **Daytime intake is robust** — solids well-established (post-6 mo), or formula/breast intake matches 24h needs in ~5 daytime feeds.
- **Baby falls asleep independently** — without feed-to-sleep association.

If all three are met and night feeds persist, you are probably maintaining a learned association rather than meeting nutritional need.

## Methods

### Method 1: Cold turkey (with extinction sleep training)

Pediatrician confirms readiness. Stop offering night feeds entirely. Continue any sleep-training method. Most infants drop the night feed within 3–5 nights once it's not on offer.

**Timeline**: 3–7 nights.

**Best for**: families who have already sleep-trained and are ready to consolidate.

### Method 2: Gradual reduction by ounce or by minute

Each night, reduce the bottle by 0.5–1 oz (formula) or shorten the nursing session by 1–2 min. After 7–10 nights, the feed is small enough to drop entirely.

**Timeline**: 7–14 nights.

**Best for**: families who want gradual; sensitive babies; families maintaining breastfeeding day-feeds and concerned about supply impact.

### Method 3: Time-stretching

Gradually push the first night feed later. If she normally feeds at midnight, intervene with non-feed soothing for 15 min the first night, 30 min the second, etc. Eventually the feed gets pushed past natural waketime.

**Timeline**: 1–3 weeks.

**Best for**: babies feeding 2+ times a night; gradual approach.

## What to keep in mind

- **Mother's supply** if breastfeeding — sudden night-feed cessation can cause engorgement, plugged ducts, mastitis. Pump small amounts to relieve pressure if needed; do not pump fully (signals supply to maintain).
- **Hunger vs. comfort**: if she's just woken once and won't soothe, that's typically comfort-seeking; if she's woken at the same exact times nightly with effective swallowing, it may be habit-driven hunger that resets when the schedule does.
- **Growth check**: weigh at next well-visit after weaning to confirm no inadvertent caloric drop.

## Watch-fors

- `[low concern]` 1–2 nights of crying during transition.
- `[monitor]` Persistent crying >7 nights without improvement.
- `[call within 24h]` Weight loss; reduced wet diapers; persistent feeding refusal during the day.
- `[call now]` Severe maternal mastitis signs (fever + breast redness + flu-like symptoms).
