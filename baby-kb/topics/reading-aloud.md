# Reading Aloud

## The evidence base

Reading aloud to infants and young children is one of the highest-effect-size interventions known for early language outcomes. The Reach Out and Read program (AAP-endorsed) has accumulated strong evidence that pediatrician-delivered book distribution + parent counseling improves expressive vocabulary and early literacy[1]. Whitehurst et al. (1988) seminal work on dialogic reading showed effect sizes of 0.5–1.0 SD for expressive vocabulary in 2–3 year-olds receiving dialogic-reading exposure vs. controls[2].

## "1000 books before kindergarten"

The popular framing — read 1000 books before kindergarten — is operationally a one-book-per-day cadence from birth (1 book × 365 days × 5 years ≈ 1825 books, well past the goal). The library card is the budget-friendly route; books rotate, repetition still happens with favorites.

## Reading by age — what to actually do

### 0–3 months
- Read at any time. Adult-directed material works (newspaper, novel, your work documents). The prosody and cadence are the active ingredients.
- High-contrast board books (*Black on White* by Tana Hoban) for visual development.
- Daily target: 5–15 minutes cumulative, distributed.

### 3–6 months
- Board books with rhythmic text (*Brown Bear, Brown Bear, What Do You See?*, *Goodnight Moon*).
- She'll start to grab books — that's engagement, not destruction. Cloth and silicone books for chewing.
- Daily target: 15–20 minutes.

### 6–9 months
- Begin pointing and naming routine: "Where's the dog? Yes, the dog."
- Lift-the-flap books (*Where Is Baby's Belly Button?*, Karen Katz series).
- Daily target: 20–30 minutes.

### 9–12 months
- Dialogic reading begins to be tractable.
- "Concept books" (colors, shapes, animals).
- Books with single words per page (*First 100 Words*) for explicit naming practice.
- Daily target: 30 minutes.

### 12+ months
- Full dialogic reading (PEER framework, see `topics/language-exposure.md`).
- Longer narratives with simple plots.
- Repetition strongly supports vocabulary acquisition — 5+ readings of the same book before "putting it away" is a useful heuristic.

## Dialogic reading — PEER framework

When reading with a 12+ month-old:
- **P**rompt: ask a question about the picture. ("What's that?")
- **E**valuate: respond to her attempt. ("Yes!" or scaffold if wrong: "It's a doggie.")
- **E**xpand: add information. ("A brown doggie. The doggie has floppy ears.")
- **R**epeat: ask her to say the new word/phrase.

Use sparingly — not every page needs PEER. Mix with simple read-through.

## Book quality matters less than expected

The research findings on dialogic reading replicate across "high quality" and "lower quality" children's books. The active ingredient is the parent-child interaction, not the book's pedagogical sophistication. That said:

**Recommended baseline collection** for year 1:
- *Goodnight Moon* (Margaret Wise Brown)
- *Brown Bear, Brown Bear, What Do You See?* (Bill Martin Jr / Eric Carle)
- *The Very Hungry Caterpillar* (Eric Carle)
- *Where Is Baby's Belly Button?* (Karen Katz)
- *Press Here* (Hervé Tullet)
- *Hippos Go Berserk!* (Sandra Boynton)
- *Each Peach Pear Plum* (Janet & Allan Ahlberg)
- *We're Going on a Bear Hunt* (Michael Rosen)
- *First 100 Words* / *First 100 Animals* (Roger Priddy)
- A book in each parent's heritage language if bilingual

## Library systems

- Most US public libraries issue cards from birth.
- "Storytime" programs — many libraries offer 0–18 mo and toddler storytimes. Free, weekly, social. Useful for both reading exposure and parent-baby socialization.
- E-book/audiobook borrowing (Libby, Hoopla) — Hoopla has children's audiobook content for 12+ mo.

## What doesn't work

- Reading apps under 18 mo. AAP discourages all screen exposure under 18 mo except video chat. Reading from a screen is screen exposure, even if pedagogical.
- "Speed reading" through 5 books in 2 minutes. Engagement matters more than count.
- Insisting on completion. If she wants to flip back, flip back. If she wants to stop, stop.

## Watch-fors

- `[low concern]` Different favorite books week-to-week; chewing on books; insisting on the same book 50 times.
- `[monitor]` No interest in books AND no interest in pointing/naming AND limited joint attention by 12–15 mo — flag at well visit.
