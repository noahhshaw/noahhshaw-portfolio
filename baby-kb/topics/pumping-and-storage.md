# Pumping and Storage

## Equipment

- **Double electric pump**: covered by ACA-mandated insurance benefit. Spectra S1/S2 (Wirecutter pick), Medela Pump In Style, or Elvie/Willow if discrete portability is the priority.
- **Hands-free pumping bra** — non-negotiable for productivity if pumping while doing anything else.
- **Pump parts** (flanges, valves, membranes, connectors). Replace per manufacturer schedule (membranes often weekly with heavy use).
- **Storage bags** (Lansinoh, Medela). Pre-labeled with date/oz.
- **Cooler bag with ice packs** for transport.

## Sizing flanges

The default flange that ships with most pumps (24mm or 27mm) is too large for many women. Wrong size = pain + reduced output. A lactation consultant or the manufacturer's sizing guide can help. Symptoms of wrong size: nipple pain, partial nipple elongation into the tunnel, white blanching post-pump.

## Storage rules (CDC)[1]

| Storage | Freshly pumped | Thawed |
|---|---|---|
| Room temp (≤77°F) | 4 hours | 1–2 hours |
| Refrigerator (≤40°F) | 4 days | 24 hours |
| Freezer (≤0°F) | 6 months optimal, 12 months acceptable | Do not refreeze |

- Do not microwave milk. Warm in warm water bath or under running warm water.
- Do not heat to >104°F — destroys enzymes and antibodies.
- Combine same-day pumped milk only after both are chilled to same temp; don't combine warm fresh with refrigerated.

## Building a freezer stash

Goal for return to work: ~2 oz frozen for each missed feed, plus 1–2 day buffer.

Strategy:
- Start at 4–6 weeks postpartum if breastfeeding is established. Pump once after the first morning feed (highest supply window) for an extra 1–3 oz.
- Store in 2–4 oz portions (matches typical bottle size; minimizes waste).

## Pump schedule when away from baby

- **Match feed frequency**. If baby normally feeds every 3 hours, pump every 3 hours.
- **Each session 15–20 min** (not longer — diminishing returns).
- **At work**: 3 sessions per 8h day is typical (e.g., 9:30, 12:30, 15:30).
- **PUMP Act 2022** entitles nursing employees to reasonable break time + private (non-bathroom) space for up to 1 year postpartum[2].

## Common problems

**Low output**: try power-pumping (10 min on, 10 min rest, 10 min on, 10 min rest, 10 min on) once daily for 3–5 days. Confirm flange size. Increase fluid intake. Consider lactation consultant.

**Plugged duct**: warm compress + massage + frequent emptying of that breast first. Sunflower lecithin (1200 mg 3–4× daily) reduces recurrence.

**Mastitis**: redness + warmth + flu-like symptoms = call OB or PCP same day. Antibiotics typically required; continue breastfeeding from affected side.

## Watch-fors (mother)

- `[low concern]` Brief soreness, milk sluggish first letdown.
- `[monitor]` Persistent low output despite optimal pumping.
- `[call within 24h]` Plugged duct that doesn't resolve in 24h with massage/heat.
- `[call now]` Mastitis: fever + breast redness + flu-like symptoms.
