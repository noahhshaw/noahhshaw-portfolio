# Nutrition — Year One Overview

## Caloric needs (rough)

| Age | Total daily kcal | Source mix |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 mo | 100–110 kcal/kg/day | Breast milk or formula 100% |
| 4–6 mo | 90–100 kcal/kg/day | Breast/formula 100% (or pre-solids) |
| 6–9 mo | 80–90 kcal/kg/day | Breast/formula ~80%, solids ~20% |
| 9–12 mo | 80 kcal/kg/day | Breast/formula ~50%, solids ~50% |
| 12+ mo | 70–80 kcal/kg/day | Whole milk + table foods |

Translates roughly to 24–32 oz/day of milk by 6 mo, decreasing as solids increase to 16–24 oz/day at 12 mo.

## Key micronutrients

- **Iron** — supplement at 4 mo for exclusively breastfed term infants per AAP (1 mg/kg/day) until iron-rich solids are established. Iron-fortified cereal + meat purées + lentils.
- **Vitamin D** — 400 IU/day for exclusively/predominantly breastfed infants. Formula-fed infants taking >32 oz/day get enough.
- **DHA** — present in formula and breast milk; not separately supplemented.
- **Iodine** — present in formula; breastfed infants get from mother (iodized salt diet).
- **Zinc** — present in breast milk; meat in solids supports continued status.

## What NOT to give

- **Honey before 12 mo** — botulism. Hard rule.
- **Cow's milk as a primary drink before 12 mo** — low iron, kidney load. Yogurt and cheese in small amounts from 6 mo onward are fine.
- **Fruit juice before 12 mo** — AAP recommends none[1].
- **Added sugar before 24 mo** per Dietary Guidelines.
- **Added salt before 12 mo** — kidney load.
- **Choking-hazard shapes**: whole nuts, popcorn, hot dogs (whole or cross-section coins), grapes (whole), cherries with pits, hard raw vegetables, gum, hard candy, marshmallows.

## What to give a lot of

- **Iron-rich**: iron-fortified cereal, meat (beef, chicken, lamb), beans/lentils, dark leafy greens cooked.
- **Healthy fats**: avocado, olive oil drizzles, full-fat yogurt, salmon.
- **Variety of textures**: progressing through year 1 from purée → mashed → soft chopped → table-food forms.
- **Variety of flavors**: every culture's foods. Spices in moderation OK from start (cumin, cinnamon, oregano, mild curry). Salt is the issue, not flavor.

## Family-meal frame

Druckerman's *Bringing Up Bébé* documents the French model: babies sit at the table with the family, eat family food (sized appropriately), and eat at family meal times. Evidence on long-term taste-acceptance breadth supports this approach[2]. The high-chair-at-table from 6 mo onward does substantial work for both nutritional variety and language exposure.

## Iron deficiency — the most-likely deficiency in year 1

- Peak risk: 9–24 mo.
- Pediatrician will check hemoglobin at 9-mo and 12-mo visits.
- Symptoms when severe: pallor, fatigue, irritability, picky eating, pica (eating non-foods).
- Treatment: oral ferrous sulfate per pediatrician dosing.

## Vegetarian/vegan considerations

Vegetarian (with dairy/egg) is fully feasible in year 1 with attention to iron (legumes + vitamin C pairing) and B12 (dairy/egg sufficient). Vegan year-one diets require careful supplementation (B12, iron, omega-3 ALA→DHA conversion is poor in infants) and pediatric dietitian consultation is recommended.

## Food allergies

Most peanut, egg, milk, wheat, soy allergies present in the first year. See `topics/introducing-solids.md` for the introduction protocol. If allergy emerges:
- Hives only (no breathing/swallowing involvement): pediatrician within 24h. Stop the food.
- Anaphylaxis: 911.
- Confirmed allergy: allergist referral; epi-pen prescribed; food avoidance.

About 80% of milk and egg allergies resolve by school age; peanut and tree-nut allergies typically persist[3].

## Watch-fors

- `[low concern]` Day-to-day intake variation (40% range up or down).
- `[monitor]` Persistent food refusal lasting >2 weeks; weight stagnation.
- `[call within 24h]` New rash post-food; persistent diarrhea > 5 days.
- `[call now]` Anaphylaxis post-food; choking with no air movement.
