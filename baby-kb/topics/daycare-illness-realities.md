# Daycare Illness Realities

## What to expect

Infants entering daycare in year 1 typically experience 8–12 illnesses across the first 12 months of attendance. The first 2–3 months are the worst — fresh exposure to a constellation of viruses the immune system hasn't seen.

Most are URIs (runny nose, cough, low-grade fever), GI illnesses (1–2 days of vomiting and/or diarrhea), or hand-foot-mouth/coxsackievirus (5–10 day course). RSV and influenza in season can be more severe.

## The data

- Daycare attendance is associated with elevated illness frequency in years 1–3.
- The pattern *reverses* by school age: daycare-attended kids have *fewer* illnesses in early elementary years (the "immune-priming" effect).
- Some studies suggest reduced asthma incidence in daycare-attended children at adolescence.
- Net first-year impact: ~8–12 days of illness for the baby; ~5–10 days of parental work absence.

## Sick policies — what's typical

Most daycares require:
- Fever ≥100.4°F → home; return after 24h fever-free without medication.
- Vomiting (>1 episode) → home; return after 24h symptom-free.
- Diarrhea (>2 episodes or contained issue) → home.
- Strep, hand-foot-mouth, conjunctivitis → home until specific criteria met.
- COVID-19 → per current local public health guidance.

## Operational planning

### Backup care

- **Identify before you need it.** Who covers when she's sent home?
  - Parent A or B taking sick days.
  - Backup nanny / family member.
  - Backup daycare (some employers offer Bright Horizons backup care benefit).
  - Grandparent on-call.
- **Sick days at work**: budget that you'll use most of yours in year 1 of daycare.

### Employer-provided benefits to use

- **Backup care**: Bright Horizons, Care.com Care@Work — many employers subsidize.
- **Dependent Care FSA**: $5,000/year pre-tax.
- **Sick days**: federal/state-mandated paid sick leave varies (CA, NYC have generous mandates).

### Reducing infection load

- **Hand-washing**: at drop-off and pick-up; re-frame as routine, not anxious.
- **No mouth/face contact** with siblings or daycare-mates' bottles, pacifiers (within reason).
- **Vaccinations current** for everyone in the household (Tdap booster for adults, flu, COVID-19).
- **Vitamin D 400 IU/day** for breastfed infants (immune support).

## When to keep her home (even if symptoms below threshold)

- Excessive fussiness disproportionate to objective signs.
- Reduced feeding for >12h.
- Unusual lethargy.
- The "something's not right" intuition with no specific finding — trust this once or twice.

## Mental framing for parents

The first 2–3 months of daycare are hard. Multiple illnesses, missed work, pediatrician visits, parent illness from baby exposure. This is expected; it does not mean the choice was wrong. By month 4–6 of daycare, the illness frequency drops sharply.

## Watch-fors

- `[low concern]` 8–12 URIs in year 1.
- `[monitor]` Each illness lasting >2 weeks; failure to thrive concerns; persistent cough >4 weeks.
- `[call within 24h]` Multiple illnesses with weight loss; persistent ear infections (>3 in 6 months); persistent wheezing post-illness.
- `[call now]` Severe respiratory distress; lethargy disproportionate; signs of dehydration.
