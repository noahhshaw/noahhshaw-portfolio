# Baby-Proofing Timeline

## Phased approach by mobility milestone

### Phase 1 — Birth to ~3 months (pre-mobility)

Minimal proofing required. Risks are caregiver-induced (dropping, falling asleep on couch with baby, leaving on elevated surface).

- **Crib**: CPSC-compliant 2011+, no bumpers, no loose bedding.
- **Changing table**: strap or stay within arm's reach always.
- **Furniture**: don't leave baby unattended on couch/bed even pre-rolling.

### Phase 2 — 3–6 months (rolling, reaching)

- **Outlet covers** in primary rooms (nursery, living room, parents' bedroom).
- **Cord management**: blind cords with cleats; lamp/electronics cords behind furniture.
- **Furniture audit**: anything baby could roll off of, or that could fall on her.
- **Floor surface**: vacuum thoroughly; small objects on floor are tempting once she rolls and reaches.

### Phase 3 — 6–9 months (sitting, mouthing, scooting)

- **Choking-hazard sweep**: walk every room at floor level. Coins, batteries, magnets, small toy parts, dog/cat food kibble, pieces of laundry tags, dryer sheets.
- **Cabinet locks**:
  - Magnetic locks (Safety 1st Magnetic Lock Set, Tot Lok) for high-value cabinets (medications, cleaning supplies, sharp knives).
  - Adhesive cabinet latches for moderate-value cabinets.
  - Some cabinets (food, low-value items) can stay accessible for exploration.
- **Toilet locks**: drowning hazard.
- **Stair gates**: at the top of any stairs, hardware-mounted (not pressure-mounted). At the bottom, pressure-mounted is acceptable.
- **Bathroom and kitchen doors**: closed when not in use.
- **Houseplants**: identify any toxic species (lily, philodendron, pothos, dieffenbachia — see ASPCA list, mostly aimed at pets but applies). Move to inaccessible heights.

### Phase 4 — 9–12 months (crawling, cruising, pulling-to-stand)

- **Furniture anchors**: TV anchored to wall (TV tip-overs cause infant deaths annually). Bookshelves anchored. Tall dressers anchored — especially IKEA-style particle-board furniture, which has caused fatal tip-overs.
- **Soft corner padding** on coffee tables, fireplace hearths.
- **Fireplace surround**: hearth padding or full surround for live-fire fireplaces.
- **Window guards**: in any window above ground level. Don't rely on screens (not weight-bearing).
- **Cords**: anything within reach gets bundled or moved.
- **Door pinch guards**: on doors she can close on her fingers.
- **Refrigerator lock** if she's reaching the handle.

### Phase 5 — 12+ months (walking, climbing)

- **Re-audit at her new height.** Things on coffee tables, end tables, low shelves now reachable.
- **Climbing-proof**: anchor anything that could be climbed (bookshelves, dressers, even toy chests).
- **Pool fencing** (if applicable): 4-ft minimum, self-latching, isolation fencing optimal.
- **Driveway/garage safety**: never reverse without confirming her location; parking-brake up.

## Specific high-risk items

### Button batteries

The single highest-risk household item for infants. Ingestion of a button battery (round, coin-size) can cause esophageal burns within 2 hours and is potentially fatal. Sources: car key fobs, hearing aids, kitchen scales, thermometers, remote controls, musical greeting cards, toys (especially novelty/imported toys).

**Prevention**:
- Inventory all devices in the home with button batteries.
- Tape over battery compartments.
- Keep spare batteries in inaccessible locked storage.
- Check toys for secure battery compartments.

**If suspected ingestion**: ER immediately. Do not wait for symptoms.

### Magnets (especially neodymium)

If two or more rare-earth magnets are swallowed, they attract through bowel walls, causing perforation. Sources: Buckyballs (banned but still in households), magnetic toy parts, refrigerator magnets if very small.

**If suspected ingestion**: ER same-day.

### Liquid laundry pods (Tide Pods, etc.)

Concentrated detergent in chewable form. Cause severe chemical burns to mouth/esophagus. **Use traditional liquid or powder detergent during the years 0–4.**

### Small magnets in toys (decorative magnetic balls, science kits)

Recall lists posted at CPSC.gov; check toys against recall database periodically.

### Cords (window blinds, electrical)

Strangulation. Use cordless blinds or cleat cord wraps.

### Furniture tip-overs

Anchor everything tall. Cliché but the deaths are preventable and recurring.

## Ongoing audit

- **Quarterly walk-through** at floor level (and later at toddler height).
- **Recall checks**: subscribe to CPSC recall alerts.
- **Battery inventory** every 3–6 months.
- **Update for new toys/gifts** — grandparent gifts often pre-date current safety standards.

## Watch-fors

- `[call within 24h]` Suspected ingestion of pill, plant, household chemical → Poison Control 1-800-222-1222 first; many resolve with phone advice. Emergency if symptoms develop.
- `[call now]` Suspected button battery ingestion; suspected magnet ingestion (multiple); chemical burn to mouth; choking with no air movement; furniture tip-over with crush injury.
