# First Birthday Planning

## Frame the day realistically

The first birthday is for the parents and grandparents more than the baby. A 12-month-old has no concept of birthday; she'll experience it as: people in the house, cake she eats with her hands, photos. Plan accordingly.

## Three formats

### Family-only (small, in-home)

- **Guest list**: 6–12 (immediate family + 1–2 close friends).
- **Time**: 60–90 minutes around morning or post-second-nap awake window.
- **Cost**: $300–800 (cake, decorations, food, cleanup).
- **Logistics**: minimal coordination; her routine intact.

### Mid-size party (in-home or rented small space)

- **Guest list**: 15–30, including kids of similar age and friends.
- **Time**: 90–120 min.
- **Cost**: $1,500–4,000 typical; venue rental in NYC/SF pushes higher.
- **Logistics**: catering or potluck; entertainment optional (musician, balloon person — none needed for a 12-mo-old).

### Large event (venue-rented)

- **Guest list**: 50+.
- **Time**: 2–3 hr.
- **Cost**: $5,000–15,000+ (NYC/SF).
- **Logistics**: photographer, full catering, decorations. Often coincides with cultural celebrations (e.g., dohl/dol for Korean families, annaprasana for Indian families).
- **Note**: many cultures have specific first-birthday traditions; the operational planning is the same, the meaning is enriched.

## Timing the day

- **Morning party** (10:00–12:00): after first nap, before second.
- **Afternoon party** (14:00–16:00): after lunch + second nap.
- **Avoid late afternoon** (16:30–18:00) — overtiredness collides with bedtime routine.
- **Birthday on a workday**: do small celebration on the day, big party the following weekend.

## Smash cake

The "smash cake" tradition: small individual cake the baby can pick up and eat (with her hands) for photos. Optional. Some parents skip — sugar exposure is otherwise minimal in year 1, and the smash cake is a 30-min spike.

If doing:
- Order from a local bakery (lead time 2–3 weeks).
- Schedule in good light, ~30 min before guests arrive (or first thing on the day-before for a clean photo without distractions).
- Nudity for cleanup or a designated "cake outfit" you don't mind tossing.

Alternative: fruit "cake" stacked rounds of watermelon, cake decorated with whipped cream — same photo, less sugar.

## Photographer

- **Booking lead time**: 2–4 months in NYC/SF for top photographers.
- **Cost**: $400–1,500 for 60–90 min session.
- **Style**: candid documentary > posed for 1-yr-olds. Posed is a coin flip — she may sit, may run.
- **Family photo**: book a separate 15-min slot for everyone-in-frame; the cake-smash dominates otherwise.

## Cake

- Local bakery custom: $80–200 for a 6–8" round + smash cake.
- Grocery store: $30–60.
- Home bake: lower direct cost; higher time cost; risk-tolerance dependent.

## Gifts

The "no gifts" or "books instead of cards" framing is increasingly common — avoids accumulating unneeded toys. Alternatives:
- 529 contributions (link from honeyfund.com or directly to plan).
- Book exchange (each guest brings a favorite children's book).
- Charitable donation in the baby's name.

If accepting gifts: maintain a tally for thank-you notes. Even if you don't write them now, the list is useful for year-end recap and grandparent acknowledgments.

## Year-1 photo book / video

Many parents compile a year-1 retrospective:
- **Photo book**: Artifact Uprising, Mixbook, Chatbooks.
- **Video**: monthly clips compiled into 12-month montage; 1Second Everyday app or similar.
- **Print stack**: monthly Polaroid/Instax against fixed backdrop; framed 12-up at year 1.

## Watch-fors on the day

- `[low concern]` Overstimulation, brief crying with too many faces; shorter nap on party day.
- `[monitor]` Sustained crying disproportionate to context.
- `[call within 24h]` Allergic reaction to cake (egg, dairy, wheat, peanut traces).
- `[call now]` Anaphylaxis from food; choking on cake (well-mashed safest); severe respiratory distress.
