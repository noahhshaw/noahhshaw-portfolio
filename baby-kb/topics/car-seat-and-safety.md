# Car Seat and Safety

## The seat itself

### Year-1 setup: rear-facing infant car seat

- **Rear-facing only**, full year and beyond. AAP recommends rear-facing until child reaches the maximum height/weight allowed by the seat manufacturer — typically 35–40 lbs and 32–40 inches, age 2+ for many infants[1].
- **Infant-only carriers** (Chicco KeyFit, Nuna Pipa, UPPAbaby Mesa) snap into a base; convenient for in-and-out but limited to ~30 lbs / 30 inches. Most outgrow before age 1.
- **Convertible seats** (Britax, Graco, Nuna Rava) install permanently rear-facing first, then convert to forward-facing later. Usable from birth in most models.

### Installation

- **Tightness**: at the belt path, the seat should not move >1 inch side-to-side or front-to-back.
- **Recline**: per manufacturer; most have an indicator. Level when baby is small (<6 months) is critical to airway.
- **Harness**: chest clip at armpit level; harness snug enough that you can't pinch slack vertically at the shoulder strap.
- **Coats**: do not buckle baby in a puffy coat. Compression in a crash creates dangerous slack. Buckle in clothes, then put coat over the harness backwards or use a car-seat-safe poncho.
- **Latch vs seatbelt**: either works if installed correctly. Most seats have weight limits for LATCH (typically 65 lbs combined seat + child); after that, seatbelt installation.
- **Local CPST**: certified child passenger safety technician will check installation free at most fire stations and AAA offices. Take advantage at least once.

## When to upgrade

- **Infant carrier outgrown**: when head is within 1 inch of the top of the seat shell, OR child reaches max weight. Move to convertible rear-facing.
- **Rear-facing → forward-facing**: AAP recommends keeping rear-facing as long as possible (typically age 2–4). Each year rear-facing reduces fatal injury risk substantially.
- **Forward-facing → booster**: ~age 4 typically; depends on harness limits.
- **Booster → no booster**: when child fits adult belt correctly (typically 4'9" tall, age 8–12).

## Other safety topics, year 1

### Sleep safety

See `topics/sleep-newborn-fundamentals.md` for ABC sleep rules. Hard summary: Alone, on Back, in Crib. No bumpers, no blankets, no positioners.

### Babyproofing — by mobility milestone

- **Pre-rolling (0–4 mo)**: minimal proofing needed. Don't leave baby on elevated surface.
- **Rolling (4–7 mo)**: never leave on couch/bed unsupervised. Outlet covers in baby's primary rooms.
- **Crawling (6–10 mo)**: full proofing. Outlet covers everywhere. Stair gates top + bottom (hardware-mounted at top). Cabinet locks (medications, cleaning supplies — magnetic locks like Safety 1st Magnetic Lock Set). Toilet locks. Small-object audit. Fireplace surrounds. Blind cord cleats — strangulation hazard.
- **Cruising/walking (9–14 mo)**: TV anchored. Bookshelves anchored. Dressers anchored (IKEA tip-over deaths preventable). Soft padding on sharp furniture corners. Pool fencing if relevant.

### Choking prevention

See `topics/choking-hazards.md` for full list. Quick rules:
- No choking-shaped foods until age 4 (whole grapes, popcorn, hot dogs, hard candy, chunks of nuts, etc.).
- Anything that fits through a toilet paper tube is a choking risk for under-3.
- Magnets (especially neodymium / Buckyballs) are life-threatening if multiple swallowed.
- Button batteries are emergent — go to ER immediately if suspected ingestion.

### Burns/scalds

- Set water heater to 120°F max.
- Test bath water temp before placing baby in (32–37°C / ~98°F target).
- Stove guard/back-burner-only when cooking with baby in kitchen.

### Drowning

- Never leave baby alone in bath even briefly. Drowning happens in 2 inches of water.
- Pool fencing if you have a pool — 4 ft minimum, self-closing/self-latching gate, isolation fencing (separating pool from house) optimal.
- Bathtub seat is not safety equipment — it's a positioner. Constant supervision still required.

### Pets

- Most household pets accommodate a baby fine, but supervised always. Dog bites happen from familiar dogs; cat scratches around eyes are most-common ER cat-related issue.
- Cribs and pet sleep zones separated.
- Pet vaccinations current (rabies, plus species-appropriate).

### Sun

- Under 6 mo: keep out of direct sun; use shade and clothing. Sunscreen only on small areas (face, hands) if shade not possible.
- 6+ mo: SPF 30+ mineral sunscreen (zinc oxide / titanium dioxide).

### Outdoor — strollers and carriers

- Stroller: certified to ASTM F833. Secure baby with provided harness. Don't hang heavy bags from the handle (tip risk).
- Carrier: AAP TICKS guidance — Tight, In view at all times, Close enough to kiss, Keep chin off the chest, Supported back. Wraps (Boba, Solly) and structured carriers (Ergobaby, BabyBjörn) both safe if used correctly.

## Watch-fors

- `[call within 24h]` Suspected button battery ingestion → ER same-day.
- `[call now]` Choking with no air movement; near-drowning even if "fine" afterward (delayed pulmonary edema possible); head injury with persistent vomiting/loss of consciousness; severe burn (>1 inch on infant) or any burn to face/genitals.
