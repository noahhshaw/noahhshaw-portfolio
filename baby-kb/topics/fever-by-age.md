# Fever — By Age, Severity Thresholds

## Definition

Fever in a child is rectal temperature ≥100.4°F (38.0°C). This threshold is the binding clinical reference. Axillary and oral routes are less accurate; in infants under 3 months, rectal is the standard.

## Why age matters

Infants <90 days have immature immune systems and limited ability to localize/clear bacterial infections. Bacteremia (bloodstream infection) and bacterial meningitis can present with fever as the only symptom, and progression can be rapid. The AAP 2021 Clinical Practice Guideline establishes specific evaluation pathways[1].

## Severity by age — actionable thresholds

### 0–28 days (newborn period)

- **Any fever ≥100.4°F rectal** → ER same-day. Full sepsis workup standard (blood, urine, often LP). No exceptions, even if immediately post-vaccine.
- **Any hypothermia (<97.7°F rectal) with poor feeding/lethargy** → same urgency.

### 29–60 days

- **Fever ≥100.4°F rectal** → call pediatrician same-day; many will direct to ER. Workup pathway depends on clinical appearance and risk factors per AAP CPG.
- Pediatrician may use risk-stratification (Step-by-Step, PECARN, Rochester) to determine workup intensity.

### 61–90 days

- **Fever ≥100.4°F rectal** → same-day evaluation. AAP CPG provides stratification framework. Most well-appearing infants get urinalysis + blood work; LP only if abnormal.

### 3–6 months

- **Fever ≥100.4°F rectal**: call within 24h if persistent, sooner if other concerning features.
- **Fever >102°F + lethargy/poor feeding**: same-day evaluation.
- **Fever lasting >48h without source**: call.
- Most infections in this age are viral; common workup includes urinalysis if no obvious viral source.

### 6–12 months

- **Fever <102°F**, looks well, eating/drinking, alert: monitor at home.
- **Fever 102–104°F**, looks well: monitor; treat with weight-based acetaminophen or ibuprofen (ibuprofen approved from 6 mo).
- **Fever >104°F** OR **lethargy/dehydration/persistent vomiting** OR **fever >72h without obvious source**: call within 24h.
- **Febrile seizure** (typical 6 mo–5 yr; brief generalized seizure with rising fever): call same-day or 911 if first seizure or seizure >5 min.

## Treatment

### Fever-reducing medication

Treat for comfort, not for the fever itself. Mild-moderate fever helps the immune response and is not by itself dangerous.

- **Acetaminophen** (Tylenol): from birth, weight-based dosing. Pediatrician will provide dosing chart at 1-mo or 2-mo visit.
- **Ibuprofen** (Motrin/Advil): from 6 months, weight-based dosing.
- **Alternating** acetaminophen and ibuprofen is sometimes recommended for high persistent fever — pediatrician should advise.
- **Aspirin**: never give to children under 19 with fever (Reye syndrome risk).

### Hydration

The biggest fever-related risk in this age group is dehydration. Continue breast/formula feeds; offer extra in small amounts. Watch wet diapers — at least 1 every 6–8h is the floor.

### Sponging, cool baths

Tepid sponging is fine; ice baths or alcohol rubs are not — induce shivering, raising core temp.

## What's NOT a fever (common misreads)

- Baby feels warm to the touch but rectal temp is <100.4°F: not a fever.
- Axillary 99.5°F (axillary readings are typically 0.5–1°F lower than rectal): borderline; check rectal.
- Post-bath warmth, post-physical-activity warmth: transient, not fever.

## Watch-fors at any age

- `[low concern]` Fever 100.4–101°F + good feeding, alert, normal play in infant >6 mo.
- `[monitor]` Fever 101–102°F + minor cold symptoms.
- `[call within 24h]` Fever ≥100.4°F at <90 days; fever >102°F + lethargy at any age; fever >72h without source.
- `[call now]` Fever + neck stiffness/bulging fontanelle (rule out meningitis); fever + petechial rash (purple spots that don't blanch — rule out meningococcemia); first febrile seizure or any seizure >5 min; fever + severe respiratory distress; lethargy disproportionate to context.
