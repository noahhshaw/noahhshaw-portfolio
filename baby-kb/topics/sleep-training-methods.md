# Sleep Training — Methods Compared

## Framing

Behavioral sleep interventions for infants are well-evidenced. The AAP, AASM, and Cochrane reviews all support them; the major RCT and 5-year follow-up data show no harm to attachment, mental health, or behavioral outcomes[1][2][3]. The disagreement among pediatric sleep specialists is not whether to do it but which method.

This file compares the five most-discussed methods. We do not pick. The right choice is a function of parental temperament, baby's temperament, and household constraints.

## Pre-conditions (all methods)

- **Baby is ≥4 months** (16 weeks adjusted age). Not earlier — circadian system and physiologic readiness are not in place.
- **Baby is healthy**: no acute illness, no recent vaccines (within 48h), no known reflux/GI issue currently flaring.
- **Sleep environment optimized**: dark room, white noise ≤50 dB, room 68–72°F, firm crib, sleep sack, no bedsharing.
- **Bedtime routine consistent** for 1+ week before starting: same order of events at the same time, anchored on baby's wake-window length.
- **Both parents on the same plan.** Mid-method method-switching prolongs the training.
- **Pediatrician aware**, especially for infants <6 mo or with reflux/feeding history.

## Method 1: Graduated Extinction (Ferber Method)

**Source**: Richard Ferber, *Solve Your Child's Sleep Problems*[4].

**Protocol**: place baby in crib drowsy-but-awake. If she cries, wait a fixed interval (e.g., 3 min) before going in. The check is brief (under 1 min), no picking up, no feeding, calm voice, then leave. Increase interval the next time (5 min, then 10 min). Subsequent nights: increase the starting interval (Night 2 starts at 5 min, etc.).

**Typical timeline**: improvement night 3, resolution night 5–7.

**Effect on parent**: moderate. Hearing crying with intervals is hard but the structured response provides a script.

**Evidence**: most-studied of all methods. Mindell 2006 AASM review: 14/14 studies positive[1].

**Best for**: parents who want a script and are not opposed to brief crying intervals; babies who escalate when parent enters the room.

## Method 2: Unmodified Extinction (Weissbluth / "Cry it Out")

**Source**: Marc Weissbluth, *Healthy Sleep Habits, Happy Child*[5].

**Protocol**: place baby in crib drowsy-but-awake at the prescribed bedtime (often earlier — Weissbluth emphasizes early bedtime, e.g., 18:00–19:00). Do not return until morning unless safety concern. If still awake when bedtime comes again the next night, repeat.

**Typical timeline**: resolution night 2–4. Fastest of all methods.

**Effect on parent**: highest in the first 1–3 nights. Crying duration peaks night 1 (often 30–60 min), drops sharply.

**Evidence**: also well-studied. Equivalent or slightly faster than graduated; equivalent on safety endpoints.

**Best for**: parents who can withstand 1–3 difficult nights for fastest resolution; babies whose crying *worsens* with parental presence (some do).

## Method 3: Chair Method (Sleep Lady Shuffle)

**Source**: Kim West / Sleep Lady tradition.

**Protocol**: parent sits in a chair next to the crib at bedtime. Provides verbal/physical reassurance (no picking up). Every 2–3 nights, the chair moves farther from the crib, until parent is outside the room.

**Typical timeline**: 1–3 weeks total.

**Effect on parent**: gentlest on parental cortisol; longest commitment.

**Evidence**: less-studied than Ferber/Weissbluth. Effect sizes appear similar but slower.

**Best for**: parents who want minimal crying; older infants (6+ mo); strong-willed babies who accept presence but not hands-on intervention.

## Method 4: Pick-Up-Put-Down

**Source**: Tracy Hogg / Baby Whisperer tradition.

**Protocol**: at bedtime, place baby in crib drowsy-but-awake. If she cries, pick up; once calm (not asleep), put down. Repeat as many times as needed. Some nights: 50+ pickups. Stop picking up once she's calm but awake.

**Typical timeline**: 1–3 weeks.

**Effect on parent**: physically tiring; emotionally easier than extinction.

**Evidence**: less-studied; appears to work for many but slower than Ferber.

**Best for**: parents who cannot tolerate any crying without intervention; babies who self-regulate quickly with brief contact.

## Method 5: Bedtime Fading

**Source**: behavioral pediatric sleep tradition.

**Protocol**: identify the baby's natural sleep onset time (e.g., 21:00). Place down at that time consistently for 3–5 nights. Once falling asleep within 15 min, move bedtime earlier by 15 min. Repeat until target bedtime is reached.

**Typical timeline**: 2–3 weeks to reach target bedtime.

**Effect on parent**: low difficulty; long timeline.

**Evidence**: moderate. Often combined with mild extinction.

**Best for**: families who don't want to push intervention; works well as a precursor or complement to other methods.

## Comparison table

| Method | Pace | Crying intensity | Parent difficulty | Best evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unmodified extinction | Fastest (2–4 nt) | Highest, briefly | Highest in nt 1–3 | Yes |
| Graduated (Ferber) | Fast (5–7 nt) | High, decreasing | Moderate | Yes — most studied |
| Chair | Slow (1–3 wk) | Low–moderate | Long commitment | Less studied |
| Pick-up-put-down | Slow (1–3 wk) | Low | Physically tiring | Less studied |
| Bedtime fading | Slow (2–3 wk) | Low | Easiest | Moderate |

## What does NOT work (consensus)

- Inconsistent response (sometimes pick up, sometimes don't). Worst possible signal.
- Method-switching mid-week. Pick one for at least 7 nights.
- Sleep training a sick baby.
- Sleep training with an active reflux issue (treat reflux first).

## Concerns parents raise

**"Will it harm attachment?"** No — Hiscock et al. Pediatrics 2012 5-year follow-up showed no differences in attachment, behavior, or maternal mood in sleep-trained vs. control children[2]. Older Middlemiss "stress hormones" paper has been criticized methodologically and not replicated[6].

**"Is it cruel?"** Cultural framing. The competing harm is sustained parental sleep deprivation, which has well-evidenced negative effects on parent mental health and infant care quality.

**"What about night feeds?"** Most infants 4–6 mo need 0–2 night feeds; 6–9 mo need 0–1; 9+ mo typically need 0. Pediatrician should confirm individual baby's nutritional needs before starting.

## Decision frame

If you want fast results and can withstand 2–3 hard nights: unmodified extinction.
If you want a structured method with the most evidence: graduated extinction (Ferber).
If you want the least crying and have 2–3 weeks: chair method.
If your baby is unusually sensitive to extinction: pick-up-put-down or bedtime fading.
