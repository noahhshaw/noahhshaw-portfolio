# Language Exposure

## What the evidence actually says

Hart and Risley's 1995 "30 million word gap" finding has been partially deflated by replication. Sperry et al. (2019) replication suggested the SES-linked gap may be closer to 4 million words in their sample[1]. The population-level magnitude is contested, but a more refined finding has held up: **conversational turns** (back-and-forth exchanges), not raw adult word count, predict later language outcomes.

Romeo et al. (2018) showed that 4–6 year old children's left inferior frontal (Broca's area) activation was driven by conversational-turn count in infancy/toddlerhood, independently of SES, IQ, and total adult word count[2]. The mechanism: language develops through bidirectional engagement, not passive exposure.

## What this means operationally

**Volume helps, turns help more.** A target of 20+ conversational turns per hour during awake time is a reasonable practical anchor. This includes:
- Babbling exchanges (you respond to her coo as if she'd spoken; pause; respond to her next coo).
- Narrating activities while she watches.
- Reading aloud with pauses for her response.
- Naming objects she points to.

## Practices that work (high-evidence)

### 1. Serve and return

When baby vocalizes (coo, babble, eventually word), respond as if she's spoken. Pause 2–3 seconds. Respond again to her next vocalization. This is the foundational pattern.

### 2. Narration

While doing ordinary tasks (laundry, cooking, walking), describe what you're doing in simple sentences. "I'm washing the apple. The water is cold. Now I'm cutting it. The apple is red inside."

### 3. Dialogic reading

Reading-aloud with active engagement. PEER framework[3]:
- **P**rompt: ask a question about the picture.
- **E**valuate: respond to her attempt.
- **E**xpand: rephrase and add information.
- **R**epeat: ask her to repeat the expansion.

Effective from ~12 mo (when she can engage); pre-cursor versions (point and name) work earlier.

### 4. Parentese (infant-directed speech)

Higher pitch, exaggerated prosody, slower pace, repetition. Ramirez-Esparza et al. (2014) showed parentese exposure at 11–14 mo predicted language production at 24 mo[4]. Use parentese without apology — it's the linguistically optimized register for early language acquisition.

## Practices that DON'T work (or harm)

### Background TV/audio

Infants exposed to background TV produce less speech themselves, and their parents talk less while it's on. AAP/multiple studies show negative association with language outcomes for under-2[5]. Even "educational" baby-targeted programming (Baby Einstein, etc.) showed slower vocabulary growth in randomized comparisons (Zimmerman et al., 2007).

### "Educational" video/apps for infants

Under 18 mo, AAP recommends none except video chat. The "video deficit" — infants don't transfer learning from video to live experience the way they do from live interaction — is well-replicated.

### Audio-only language exposure

Kuhl et al. (2003) showed live human-interaction Mandarin exposure preserved phoneme discrimination in English-environment 9-mo-olds; audio-only and video-only did not[6]. Live human interaction is the active ingredient.

## Targets by age

| Age | Practical target |
|---|---|
| 0–3 mo | Narrate; talk during feeds, bath, walks. Aim for parent talking continuously during awake time. |
| 3–6 mo | Add cooing-back exchanges; respond to her vocalizations. 10+ turns/hour awake. |
| 6–9 mo | Add naming + pointing. Read aloud daily. 20+ turns/hour. |
| 9–12 mo | Dialogic reading begins to be tractable. "Where's the…?" "What's that?" |
| 12+ mo | Expansions: she says "ball," you say "yes, the red ball." Build phrases on her 1-word utterances. |

## Receptive vs expressive

Receptive vocabulary (understands) leads expressive (says) by 1–6 months in year 1. By 12 mo, typical infant understands 50+ words, says 1–5. By 18 mo, understands ~150–200 words, says 20–50. The "vocabulary explosion" typically arrives 18–24 mo.

## Watch-fors

- `[low concern]` 12-mo with 0–3 words but rich babble and joint attention — within range.
- `[monitor]` 18 mo with 0 words: raise at 18-mo visit (M-CHAT-R/F autism screen).
- `[call within 24h]` Sudden loss of acquired words/skills.
- `[call now]` (No language-specific call-now scenarios in year 1.)
