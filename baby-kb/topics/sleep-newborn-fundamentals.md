# Sleep — Newborn Fundamentals (0–3 months)

## What's actually happening biologically

Newborns sleep 14–17 hours per 24h, distributed in 2–4-hour stretches with no diurnal preference. Active (REM-equivalent) sleep dominates 50% of total sleep time, vs. ~20% in adults. Circadian rhythm — the day/night cycle anchored by melatonin and cortisol — does not consolidate until 6–12 weeks of age[1]. This means: newborns waking every 2–3 hours at night is not a problem to fix; it's the normal physiology.

By the **end of week 12**, most term infants have begun consolidating night sleep into longer stretches (4–6 hours). The "biological transition" from neonatal sleep to adult-like 4-stage sleep architecture occurs around 4 months — the substrate of the so-called 4-month sleep regression. See `topics/sleep-4-month-regression.md`.

## Safe sleep — the binding rules

The AAP 2022 safe-sleep recommendations are not optional. Bedsharing is associated with elevated SIDS/SUID risk and the AAP recommends against it under any circumstances, including breastfeeding[2]. The actionable rules:

- **Back, every sleep.** Side-sleeping and tummy-sleeping correlate with higher SIDS risk.
- **Alone in the sleep space.** No bedsharing. No co-sleepers attached to the parental bed (the dock-a-tot is not safe-sleep compliant). A bassinet or crib next to the bed is room-sharing — explicitly recommended for at least the first 6 months.
- **In a crib** that meets current CPSC standards (post-2011). No drop-side cribs. No bumpers (federal ban took effect 2023). No loose blankets, pillows, stuffed animals, sleep positioners. Firm flat mattress with fitted sheet only.
- **Smoke-free environment.**
- **Pacifier at sleep onset** is associated with reduced SIDS risk — offer once breastfeeding is established (typically 3–4 weeks).
- **Breastfeeding** is associated with reduced SIDS — exclusive for ~6 months if possible per AAP.

Room-sharing without bedsharing reduces SIDS risk by up to 50%[3]. The 6-month duration is the AAP recommendation; some families extend to 12 months, others move baby to her own room at 4 months. Both are within community of practice.

## What sleep environment to actually buy

- **Crib or bassinet that meets CPSC 2011+ standards.** Halo Bassinest, SNOO (research-backed motion bassinet), Babybjörn Cradle, or any CPSC-compliant standard crib.
- **Firm flat mattress** that fits with no gap >2 fingers' width.
- **Fitted sheet only.** No bumper. No quilt.
- **Sleep sack** (Halo SleepSack, Kyte BABY) — replaces the loose blanket. Begin in TOG 1.0 for room temp 68–72°F.
- **Swaddle** for the first 6–8 weeks until any signs of rolling. Once she shows rolling attempts, swaddle is no longer safe — transition to arms-out swaddle (Love to Dream Swaddle Up) or sleep sack.
- **White noise machine** at ≤50 dB measured at the crib, run continuously through night sleep. Hatch Rest, Yogasleep Dohm, or similar.
- **Room-darkening shades** for daytime nap consolidation by 3–4 months.
- **Room temperature 68–72°F** (20–22°C). Overheating is a SIDS risk factor.

## Calming strategies — the 5 S's

Karp's *Happiest Baby on the Block*[4] proposes five evidence-informed soothing techniques that mimic the in-utero environment. Effect sizes are moderate but practically useful in the first 12 weeks:

1. **Swaddle** — tight, with arms inside. Recreates the womb's pressure.
2. **Side or stomach hold** — for soothing only, never for sleep.
3. **Shush** — loud "shhh" sound at the volume of a crying baby. White noise is the recorded equivalent.
4. **Swing** — small fast jiggling motion (head supported), not large slow rocking.
5. **Suck** — pacifier or finger.

Used together and in sequence, these address most newborn fussiness states. Failure to soothe should always raise the medical-cause question first (hunger, temperature, fever, illness, pain).

## Realistic expectations week-by-week

| Age | Total sleep / 24h | Longest stretch night | Number of feeds night |
|-----|---|---|---|
| 0–2 wk | 16–18h | 2–3h | 4–5 |
| 2–4 wk | 14–17h | 3–4h | 3–4 |
| 4–8 wk | 14–16h | 4–5h | 2–3 |
| 8–12 wk | 14–15h | 5–6h (some) | 1–2 |
| 12–16 wk | 13–15h | 5–8h (sleep-trained) or 2–3h (regression) | 1–2 |

These are population averages. Variance is large. A baby who sleeps 4-hour stretches at week 4 is not "ahead"; one who's still 2-hour stretches at week 8 is not "behind."

## When to call

- `[low concern]` Multiple wakings in newborn period; cluster feeding; daytime sleepiness >3h stretches.
- `[monitor]` Sleep stretches not lengthening at all by 12 weeks; persistent daytime drowsiness with poor feeding.
- `[call within 24h]` Persistent inability to wake for feeds; lethargy disproportionate to recent activity.
- `[call now]` Apnea (pause >20 sec or with color change). Cyanosis around lips during sleep.
