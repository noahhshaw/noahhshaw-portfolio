# Sleep — The 4-Month Regression

## What it actually is

The 4-month sleep regression is the only sleep regression in year 1 that is biological rather than situational. Around 14–18 weeks, infant sleep cycles shorten from the neonatal 50–60 min cycle to the adult-like ~45 min cycle, and sleep architecture differentiates into the four adult stages (N1, N2, N3, REM). The transition between cycles is now a fully alert micro-arousal — and unless the infant has learned to fall asleep independently, she calls for help to get back to sleep[1].

The functional symptom: an infant who was sleeping 6–8 hour stretches at 12 weeks now wakes 4–8 times per night and cries for parent intervention.

## Timing

- **Onset**: most commonly weeks 14–18 (days 98–126).
- **Peak**: weeks 16–20.
- **Resolution**: 2–6 weeks for most families with consistent response strategy. Without intervention, can persist months.

## Why "regression" is a misleading word

Nothing has gone backward developmentally. The baby's brain has matured. The expectation of consolidated sleep was based on 8–14 weeks of neonatal sleep, which doesn't predict post-transition sleep. Better mental model: this is a permanent change in baseline, not a temporary disruption.

## Evidence-based responses

The pediatric-sleep literature converges on three strategies, presented here in order of intervention strength.

### Option A — Maintain current practice; wait it out

Some families ride the regression without behavioral change. After 4–8 weeks, most infants self-consolidate as they accumulate experience with the new architecture. Drawbacks: parental sleep deprivation can be severe; some infants do not self-consolidate and the pattern persists into 6–9 months.

**Evidence**: low-to-moderate quality. Observational data suggests ~30–50% of infants self-resolve without intervention by 6 mo.

### Option B — Adjust sleep hygiene without formal training

Strengthen the sleep environment and routine without imposing extinction-style protocols:

- Drop a nap if she's outgrowing 4-nap structure (typical at this age — transition to 3 naps).
- Bring bedtime forward to 18:00–19:00 (overtiredness compounds night wakings).
- Tighten the bedtime routine: bath → feed → book → song → sleep, in the same order, every night.
- Drowsy-but-awake placement at bedtime: she falls asleep in the crib, not in arms.
- Room-darkening, white noise, room temp 68–72°F.

**Evidence**: moderate quality. Improves outcomes, may not fully resolve.

### Option C — Behavioral sleep training

Multiple methods, all variants of "extinction" — reducing parent intervention in response to night cries:

- **Graduated extinction (Ferber method)**: progressively longer wait intervals before checking. Most-evidenced, moderate intensity. See `topics/sleep-training-methods.md`.
- **Unmodified extinction (Weissbluth approach)**: place down awake; do not return until morning. Fastest results (typically 3–4 nights); highest parental difficulty in the first nights.
- **Chair method**: parent in the room on a chair, gradually moving farther from crib over 1–2 weeks. Gentlest on parent; longest training period.
- **Pick-up-put-down**: pick up, soothe to calm-not-asleep, put down. Repeat as needed. Mid-intensity; mid-duration.

**Evidence on safety**: 5-year follow-up data (Hiscock et al., Pediatrics 2012) showed no measurable harm to children's mental health, behavior, or attachment. Cortisol-spike studies (Middlemiss et al. 2012) have been criticized for methodology and have not replicated[2]. The AAP, AASM, and Cochrane reviews all support behavioral sleep interventions from ~4–6 mo.

**Evidence on efficacy**: 14/14 graduated-extinction studies in the AASM 2006 meta-review reported positive outcomes (reduction in night wakings, faster sleep onset)[3].

## Recommended starting age

- **Earliest**: 4 mo (16 weeks). Some practitioners say 5 mo. Below 4 mo, infants still need night feedings and the developing circadian system makes intervention less effective.
- **Latest practical**: 9 mo. Beyond this, sleep associations are deeply established and training takes longer.
- **Sweet spot for most families**: 4–6 mo, as the regression is in progress.

## What to do tonight if you're in the regression

1. Audit the sleep environment (firm crib mattress, fitted sheet, room temp 68–72°F, white noise, room-darkening).
2. Audit the routine (consistent order, drowsy-but-awake placement).
3. If both are tight and she's still waking 4+ times per night past week 18: choose a method (Option C) and commit for 7 nights. Most methods show meaningful improvement by night 5–7.
4. If choosing Option A or B, set a re-evaluation date (typically week 22–24) — if no improvement, escalate.

## Watch-fors

- `[low concern]` Multiple night wakings during the regression — biological, not behavioral.
- `[monitor]` Failure to consolidate after 6+ weeks of intervention.
- `[call within 24h]` Sleep disruption + concerning daytime symptoms (poor feeding, lethargy).
- `[call now]` Apnea or color change during sleep.
