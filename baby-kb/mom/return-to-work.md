# Return to Work

## Timing decision

US default for many corporate employees: 12-week leave, returning around day 84. Some employers offer 16, 20, or 26 weeks. State PFL (CA, NY, NJ, etc.) extends paid coverage in many cases.

Considerations for timing:
- **Breastfeeding establishment**: 12 weeks gives stable supply; 6 weeks is feasible but tighter.
- **Sleep regression**: 4-month regression hits weeks 14–18; returning before vs. during vs. after is a forcing function.
- **Daycare adjustment**: 1–2 week buffer between return and full daycare.
- **Personal**: emotionally and physically ready varies widely.

## Pre-return planning (last 2–4 weeks of leave)

### Pumping setup at work

- **Locate pumping room**: book it in your calendar 1–2 weeks before return so it's reserved.
- **Notify manager** of pumping schedule (typically 3 sessions of 15–20 min in 8h day).
- **Inform team** as needed — most colleagues will be supportive once asked.
- **Pumping bag** stocked: pump, parts (2 sets ideally), bottles/storage bags, hands-free bra, cooler with ice packs, microwave-safe sterilizer bags, snacks.
- **Backup parts** in desk drawer.

### Childcare onboarding

- **Trial-runs** of daycare/nanny for half-days, full-days, in the 1–2 weeks before return. Reduces transition shock.
- **Documentation**: emergency contacts, pediatrician, allergies, schedule, feeding instructions, diaper changes, sleep cues, comfort items.
- **First-week schedule**: confirmed; backup plan if anything falls through.

### Logistics

- **Trial-run the morning routine**: drop-off + commute + arrival on time. Identify pinch points.
- **Trial-run the evening routine**: arrival + dinner + bedtime + feeding + sleep with the actual workday version of mom (not vacation-mode mom).
- **Outsource what's outsourceable**: grocery delivery, meal kit subscription, house cleaning, dog walking. The first 2 months back are not the time to also start cooking from scratch.

### Wardrobe

- **Clothes that work for pumping**: button-front blouses, dresses with bottom-up access, nursing tops. Suit jacket layered over makes pumping breaks discrete.
- **Spare top** in office for spit-up incidents.

## First two weeks back

- **Half-throttle expected output.** Block long meetings; defer big projects 2 weeks if possible.
- **Pump time blocked** on calendar as "DO NOT BOOK" or labeled as a meeting.
- **End time defended** — pickup deadline is hard.
- **Communications expectations**: confirm with manager what response time is expected. Negotiate latitude in first 2 weeks.

## Pumping rhythm

- **Morning feed before leaving** — full feed; baby's last full breast feed before daycare.
- **Pump session 1**: ~3 hours after morning feed (typical 9:30–10:00).
- **Pump session 2**: ~12:30–13:00.
- **Pump session 3**: ~15:30–16:00.
- **Evening reunion feed**: as soon as you reunite (signals baby + supply).
- **Bedtime feed**.
- **Maybe 1 night feed** if baby still wakes.

Total feeds in 24h: 5–6 + 3 pumps = 8–9 stimulations of breast/pump. Supply maintenance.

## Common stressors

### Supply drop

Most common at week 6–8 back. Causes: missing pump sessions, longer gaps between feeds, stress, dehydration, period returning.

Address:
- Add a 4th pump session if 3 isn't holding supply.
- Power pump once daily for a week (10 min on / 10 min rest / 10 min on / 10 min rest / 10 min on).
- Confirm hydration (1 oz/lb body weight as ballpark).
- Eliminate any new supply-suppressing factors (combined hormonal contraception, pseudoephedrine).

### Mom guilt

Documented and prevalent. Sources:
- Cultural narrative that mothers should be primary caregivers full-time.
- Daycare/nanny illness exposure misattributed to "wrong choice."
- Comparison with stay-home mothers in your social circle.

What helps:
- The data: NICHD studies show high-quality non-parental care produces equivalent cognitive/social outcomes; some advantages on language and school readiness.
- A clear understanding of why working: financial, identity, modeling for the child, household stability.
- Time-with-baby protected and high-quality (mornings, evenings, weekends fully present).

### Overwhelm

The first 8 weeks back are statistically the hardest in year 1. Signs of crossing into PPD/PPA territory: see `mom/ppd-watch-fors.md`. EPDS rescreening at month 6 postpartum (around 3 months back) is reasonable.

## Childcare changes mid-year

If the original plan isn't working — daycare illness frequency overwhelming, nanny departing, schedule changes — switching mid-year is common and not damaging if the new caregiver is stable. Transition over 1–2 weeks ideally.

## Father's role at this transition

The split of childcare responsibilities should be 50/50 across drop-off, pickup, sick days, doctor visits, evening routines. The "default-parent burden" — where one parent (almost always the mother) becomes the implicit responsible-party for everything — is the largest avoidable stress driver in the return-to-work window.

Concrete: alternate sick days. Alternate pickup days. Alternate weekend morning feeds.

## Watch-fors (mother)

- `[low concern]` Mood dip first 1–2 weeks back; supply variation.
- `[monitor]` Persistent low mood beyond 2 weeks back; sleep deteriorating; recurrent plugged ducts (pumping issue or stress).
- `[call within 24h]` EPDS >10 on rescreen; persistent supply collapse; mastitis.
- `[call now]` Suicidal ideation; thoughts of harming baby; severe panic / dissociation.
