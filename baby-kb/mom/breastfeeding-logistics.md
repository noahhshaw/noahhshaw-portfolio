# Breastfeeding Logistics

This is the operations manual. The decision frame for breastfeeding vs formula is in `topics/breastfeeding-vs-formula-vs-combo.md`. The pumping mechanics are in `topics/pumping-and-storage.md`. This file covers the day-to-day breastfeeding workflow.

## First 72 hours — the establishment window

- **Skin-to-skin within first hour** of delivery if possible — supports milk-supply signaling.
- **First feed in delivery room** with lactation consultant assistance.
- **Demand-frequency in first 72h**: 8–12 feeds per 24h. Do not let baby go >3 hours without offering breast (waking baby is OK if feeding hasn't been frequent enough).
- **Both breasts per feed** in first weeks (drains supply signaling on both sides).
- **Lactation consultant consult before discharge** and again within 72h of being home.

## Latch — what to look for

**Good latch indicators**:
- Wide-open mouth with lips flanged (fish-lips).
- Tongue extended over lower gum.
- Asymmetric latch — more areola visible above than below.
- Audible swallowing (not clicking).
- Comfortable for mother (toe-curling pain = bad latch).
- Cheeks rounded, not dimpled.
- Baby's nose free for breathing.

**Bad latch signs**:
- Painful latching beyond initial 30 sec.
- Clicking sounds.
- Lips tucked in (especially lower lip).
- Sliding off nipple repeatedly.
- White-blanched nipple post-feed.
- Cracked, bleeding, or significantly sore nipples.

## Common positions

- **Cradle hold** — classic; harder with newborns.
- **Cross-cradle hold** — opposite arm supports head; better control for newborns.
- **Football hold** — baby tucked under arm; good for c-section recovery (avoids belly), large breasts, twins.
- **Side-lying** — mother and baby on side; useful for night feeds and recovery.
- **Laid-back / biological nurturing** — mother reclined, baby on chest, gravity-assisted latching.

## Feed frequency over time

| Age | Feeds/24h | Per feed |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 wk | 8–12 | 10–20 min/side |
| 2–4 wk | 8–10 | 10–25 min total |
| 1–3 mo | 7–9 | 15–25 min total |
| 3–6 mo | 6–8 | 15–20 min |
| 6–12 mo | 4–6 (+ solids) | 10–15 min |

## Output as supply indicator

In first week:
- Day 1: 1 wet diaper.
- Day 2: 2 wet diapers.
- Day 3: 3 wet diapers.
- Day 4: 4 wet diapers.
- **Day 5+**: 6+ wet diapers/day.
- Stools: meconium (black) → transitional (green-brown) → milk-stool (yellow-mustard, breastfed) by day 4–5.

After first week:
- ≥6 wet diapers/day.
- ≥3 stools/day until 4–6 weeks (then can drop to 1–2/day or even 1/several days — both are normal in breastfed infants).

## Common problems

### Engorgement (days 3–5 milk-coming-in, or any sudden unfeed gap)

- Cool compress between feeds, warm compress immediately before feeds.
- Pump just to comfort if baby can't fully drain — do NOT pump fully (signals oversupply).
- Cabbage leaves (folkloric, mild evidence; comfort).

### Plugged duct

- Hard tender lump, no fever, no flu-like symptoms.
- Frequent emptying (start with affected side); warm compress + massage during feeding.
- Dangling-feed position (baby below breast, gravity-assisted).
- Sunflower lecithin (1200 mg 3–4× daily) reduces recurrence.
- Resolves in 24–48h with intervention.

### Mastitis

- Hard lump + redness + warmth + fever + flu-like symptoms.
- **Call OB or PCP same-day**; antibiotics typically required (dicloxacillin or cephalexin).
- Continue breastfeeding from affected side — milk is safe; emptying is therapeutic.
- 2024 ABM Mastitis Spectrum protocol moved away from frequent pumping — emphasizes anti-inflammatory measures (cold, ibuprofen, gentle handling) over aggressive emptying.

### Thrush (yeast infection)

- White patches in baby's mouth that don't wipe off; sharp shooting pain in nipples post-feed; sometimes red shiny rash.
- Pediatrician: nystatin or fluconazole for baby.
- OB: topical antifungal (clotrimazole) or oral fluconazole for mother.
- Treat both simultaneously; treat 24–48h beyond symptom resolution.

### Low supply (perceived vs actual)

**Often perceived but actually fine**: baby growth, weight gain, output normal — supply is fine even if breasts feel less full.

**Actually low**: baby weight gain insufficient, output low, persistent fussiness with feeds.

Causes of actually-low supply:
- Insufficient frequency (<8 feeds/24h in newborn).
- Poor latch / inefficient transfer.
- Hormonal (thyroid, retained placental fragment, polycystic ovaries).
- Anatomic (insufficient glandular tissue, prior breast surgery).
- Medications (combination birth control, pseudoephedrine).

If suspected:
- Lactation consultant evaluation.
- Pre/post-feed weighing.
- Pump after each feed for 1 week to boost supply.
- Galactagogues: oatmeal, fenugreek (mixed evidence), domperidone (off-label, concerns about cardiac side effects).

### Oversupply

Less commonly discussed but real:
- Forceful let-down (baby pulls off, sputters).
- Green frothy stools (foremilk-hindmilk imbalance).
- Frequent plugged ducts.
- Engorgement persistent.

Manage: feed one side per feed (block feeding), express minimal amount before feeding, dangling/laid-back position to reduce flow.

## Nipple care

- **Air-dry post-feed** when possible.
- **Lanolin** (Lansinoh, Earth Mama) or **Silverette cups** between feeds.
- **Avoid soap on nipples** — disrupts the protective oils.
- **Crack/blister care**: lanolin, hydrogel pads, sometimes APNO ointment (compounded; OB-prescribed).

## Public / workplace breastfeeding

- US PUMP Act 2022 entitles nursing employees to reasonable break time + private (non-bathroom) space for up to 1 year postpartum at most workplaces.
- Public breastfeeding is legal in all 50 states. Cover or no cover is the mother's choice; both are normal.

## When to consider weaning or supplementing

- Baby's weight gain insufficient despite optimization.
- Mother's mental health is suffering significantly.
- Mother's career or schedule is unsustainable with current breastfeeding pattern.
- Medical reasons (severe mastitis recurrence, certain medications).

These are valid reasons. The breastfeeding evidence does not support staying with a regimen that is harming the mother for marginal benefit to baby.

## Watch-fors

- `[low concern]` Day-to-day variation in feed length / supply feeling; soreness improving over first 2 weeks.
- `[monitor]` Persistent latch pain; recurrent plugged ducts; supply concerns.
- `[call within 24h]` Plugged duct not resolving in 24h; nipple cracking with bleeding; baby weight gain insufficient.
- `[call now]` Mastitis (fever + breast redness + flu-like); abscess (palpable hard mass + fever); nipple infection with severe pain.
