# Week 03 — Days 14–20

## Focus
Common first growth spurt around days 14–21. Expect 24–48 hours of unusually frequent feeding ("cluster feeding"), then a reset. Mother's milk supply, if breastfeeding, calibrates to demand during this window — resist the urge to supplement reflexively, but do not ignore signs of insufficient transfer.

## Expected developmental milestones
- **Motor**: stronger head lifts in tummy time (a few seconds at a time). Hands beginning to open occasionally.
- **Sensory**: visually tracks objects across midline more consistently. Settles or alerts to familiar voices.
- **Social**: reflexive smiles continue; occasional sustained eye contact during feeds.
- **Feeding**: target 6+ wet diapers/day, ≥3 stools/day if breastfeeding (formula-fed stool frequency drops sooner — fewer per day is normal).

## Watch-fors
- `[low concern]` Cluster feeding 24–48h, fussiness in evenings, increased need to be held.
- `[low concern]` "Baby acne" (neonatal cephalic pustulosis) — peaks ~3–4 weeks, resolves on its own.
- `[monitor]` Persistent baby blues in mother (tearfulness, anxiety, sleep disruption beyond what the baby imposes). 50–80% of mothers experience baby blues; resolution by 2 weeks postpartum is typical. Persistence beyond week 2 escalates the concern toward postpartum depression — see `mom/ppd-watch-fors.md`.
- `[call within 24h]` Forceful projectile vomiting after most feeds with weight stagnation (pyloric stenosis: typically male, first-born, presents weeks 3–6, surgical correction).
- `[call now]` Fever ≥100.4°F rectal in infant <90 days. Apnea (pause >20 sec or with color change). Seizure activity.

## Action items for parents
- **EPDS (Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale)** screening for the mother around week 2–3. If pediatrician hasn't screened, take it yourself online — a score >10 warrants a call to OB or PCP. Score ≥13 traditionally distinguishes likely depression[1].
- **Open the 529 plan** now that the SSN has arrived (typical mail timing 2–4 weeks post-birth). See `dad/finances-529.md` for plan selection. Front-loading early years compounds maximally.
- **Verify health insurance enrollment processed** for baby. Call insurer; don't trust the form went through.
- **Apply for paternity leave** (if not already on it) — coordinate with employer's HR and any state-mandated paid family leave (CA: PFL up to 8 weeks; NY: PFL up to 12 weeks). See `dad/paternity-leave-optimization.md`.

## Enrichment opportunities
- **Tummy time** during awake-alert windows after feeds, accumulating 10–15 min/day. Use a rolled receiving blanket under the chest if he fatigues quickly.
- **Mirror play**: hold baby in front of a mirror for 30–60 seconds. The face-tracking response is the active ingredient — he's not yet recognizing self, but face-perception circuitry is tuning.
- **Bilingual exposure (if planned)**: by week 3 the second-language parent should already be speaking the second language consistently. Phoneme discrimination begins narrowing around 6 mo[2]; the establishment of the consistent input pattern matters more than the volume right now.

## What's coming next
- **Week 04**: 1-month well visit. First social smile typically appears.
- **Week 05**: Peak of the "PURPLE crying" / colic curve at ~6 weeks corrected age.
- **Week 06**: Maternal 6-week postpartum visit (clearance for exercise/sex usually given here).
