# Week 01 — Days 0–6 (newborn)

**Approximate calendar dates**: 2026-05-11 to 2026-05-17 (assuming term delivery at 40 weeks).

## Focus
The first week is a metabolic and feeding stabilization period. The baby is losing 5–10% of birth weight, learning to coordinate suck-swallow-breathe, clearing meconium, and the mother is recovering from delivery. Survival metrics dominate — output, feeds, jaundice, weight trajectory[1].

## Expected developmental milestones
- **Motor**: turns head side-to-side when on tummy; reflexive grasp; involuntary startle (Moro) reflex.
- **Cognitive**: fixates briefly on high-contrast patterns and faces at 8–12 inches (focal length of nursing position is not coincidental).
- **Sensory**: responds to loud sounds (newborn hearing screen will have run in hospital — confirm pass).
- **Feeding**: 8–12 feeds per 24h if breastfeeding (every 2–3 hours); 1.5–3 oz per feed every 3–4 hours if formula. Cluster feeding in evenings is normal[2].
- **Output by end of week 1**: ≥6 wet diapers/day, ≥3 stools/day. Stool transitions from black meconium → green-brown → mustard-yellow (breastfed) or tan (formula) by day 4–5.

## Watch-fors

- `[low concern]` Newborn rash (erythema toxicum), peeling skin, milia, occasional sneezes/hiccups, single black-tarry meconium stool stuck in diaper.
- `[low concern]` Weight loss up to 7% of birth weight by day 3–5, regained by day 10–14.
- `[monitor]` Weight loss 7–10% — flag at first-week visit; pediatrician will assess feeding effectiveness.
- `[call within 24h]` Yellow tinge spreading from face to chest/belly (jaundice descending caudally — bilirubin likely climbing), <6 wet diapers by day 5, persistent inability to latch, weight loss >10%.
- `[call now]` Rectal temperature ≥100.4°F (38.0°C) — in infants <90 days this is a workup-required event, full stop[3]. Lethargy, refusing all feeds, blue/grey color, breathing >60/min at rest, persistent grunting with each breath, projectile vomiting (especially bilious/green).

## Action items for parents

- **Schedule the first-week visit** (day 3–5). The hospital pediatrician will tell you to call your chosen pediatrician within 24h of discharge — make that call.
- **Confirm metabolic newborn screen and CCHD pulse-ox screen passed.** Both are state-mandated. Some metabolic conditions (PKU, MCADD, congenital hypothyroidism) have narrow treatment windows.
- **File birth certificate paperwork** before hospital discharge if possible. Social Security number arrives ~2–4 weeks later; you'll need it for the 529 plan and dependent tax claim.
- **Set up the pediatrician portal** for messaging and after-hours triage.
- **Lactation consultant on speed dial** — the first 72h post-discharge is the highest-failure-rate window for breastfeeding establishment[4].
- **Stock the postpartum supply chain**: peri bottle, ice packs, pads, stool softener (Colace), Tylenol/ibuprofen rotation per OB, nipple cream (lanolin or Silverette).

## Enrichment opportunities

- **Skin-to-skin** as the default holding mode in the first 2 weeks. Regulates baby's temperature, glucose, and breathing; supports milk supply if breastfeeding[5].
- **Read aloud** for 5–10 minutes/day. The content doesn't matter — your tax return works. The cadence and prosody are the active ingredient. This week is the start of a daily read-aloud habit you'll maintain through year 1 (target: 1000 books before kindergarten = ~1 book/day from birth).
- **High-contrast visual cards** (black/white/red, ~8 inches across) at 8–12 inches from baby's face during awake-alert windows. Supports developing visual cortex contrast sensitivity.
- **Live music**: sing or hum to him. Recorded music is fine in the background but the active ingredient — for both bonding and prosodic-pattern learning — is live human voice[6].

## What's coming next

- **Week 02**: First-week weight check (likely day 7–10 visit). Cord stump dries and falls (typically days 5–15). Cluster feeding intensifies.
- **Week 03**: Often labeled the first "growth spurt" — increased feeding frequency for ~48h, then resets.
- **Week 04**: Pediatrician 1-month visit window opens. First social smile typically emerges in week 4–6.
