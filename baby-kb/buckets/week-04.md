# Week 04 — Days 21–27

## Focus
The 1-month milestone window. The first social smile — directed at a face, in response to a face, not just a reflexive gas-smile — typically emerges between week 4 and week 6. The pediatrician will look for it at the 1-month visit. This is also the start of the peak-crying curve (5–6 weeks corrected age).

## Expected developmental milestones
- **Motor**: holds head up briefly when on tummy; arm and leg movements smoother and more symmetric.
- **Cognitive/sensory**: prefers human faces over other patterns. Recognizes parents' voices.
- **Language/communication**: makes sounds other than crying (CDC 2-month milestone); some infants reach this earlier[1].
- **Social/emotional**: first social smile; calms when picked up by familiar caregiver.
- **Feeding**: 6–8 feeds per 24h becoming more typical for breastfed infants; formula-fed at 3–4 oz every 3–4 hours.

## Watch-fors
- `[low concern]` Increasing fussiness in evenings ("witching hour" 5–8pm); cradle cap (yellow scale on scalp); blocked tear duct (continuous tearing one eye, no redness).
- `[monitor]` Crying >3 hours/day, >3 days/week, >3 weeks fits the classic Wessel definition of colic — peaks 6–8 weeks, resolves by 12–16 weeks. Coping strategies more useful than diagnostics, but rule out cow's-milk-protein intolerance if other GI signs present.
- `[call within 24h]` Forceful projectile vomiting + weight loss (pyloric stenosis). Persistent inability to console + arching back during feeds (severe GERD vs. CMPI).
- `[call now]` Fever ≥100.4°F rectal. Cyanosis around lips with crying. Seizure-like activity.

## Action items for parents
- **Schedule 1-month well visit** if not already done (window day 28–35).
- **Prepare for 1-month visit**: bring feeding log, output log, any concerns. The pediatrician will plot growth on WHO curves (preferred for breastfed infants under 2 yr; CDC curves for older children).
- **Begin researching daycare/nanny** seriously if return to work is at 12 or 16 weeks. Top-tier daycares in NYC and SF have 6–18 month waitlists; nanny searches typically take 4–8 weeks.
- **Order car seat #2** if you have a second vehicle and haven't yet.
- **Mother's 6-week postpartum visit** with OB approaching — pre-write your questions: lingering pelvic floor symptoms, mood, sex/contraception, exercise return.

## Enrichment opportunities
- **Tummy time**: 15–20 minutes/day cumulative across short bouts. Lay him on your chest at a slight incline to make it easier in the early weeks.
- **Daily read-aloud** (10–15 min). At this age, board books with high-contrast images and rhythmic text are ideal: *Black on White* by Tana Hoban, *Brown Bear, Brown Bear*, *Goodnight Moon*.
- **Conversational turns**: when he vocalizes (even a coo), respond as if he'd spoken, pause, then respond again to his next vocalization. This is the foundational pattern Romeo et al. (2018) showed predicts later language outcomes[2].

## What's coming next
- **Week 05**: Peak crying curve approaching. 1-month well visit may fall here.
- **Week 06**: Mother's 6-week OB postpartum visit.
- **Week 07**: Crying typically beginning to taper from peak. Start thinking about 2-month vaccines.
