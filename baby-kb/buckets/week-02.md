# Week 02 — Days 7–13

## Focus
Weight regain and feeding-pattern stabilization. By end of week 2 most term infants have regained birth weight. The mother is in lochia rubra-to-serosa transition[1]. Hospital is firmly behind you; the rhythm of life is now feed → diaper → 60–90 min sleep → repeat, around the clock.

## Expected developmental milestones
- **Motor**: brief head lifts during tummy time. Hands fisted most of the time.
- **Cognitive/sensory**: tracks moving high-contrast object briefly across midline.
- **Social**: occasional reflexive smile (true social smiling typically week 4–6, but pre-cursors emerge).
- **Feeding**: 8–12 breastfeeds per 24h still standard; formula intake creeping toward 2–3 oz per feed every 3–4 hours.

## Watch-fors

- `[low concern]` Cord stump dries and falls between days 5–15. A small spot of blood when it detaches is normal.
- `[low concern]` "Witch's milk" (small breast bud secretion) and pseudo-menstruation in girls from maternal hormone withdrawal — resolves in 1–2 weeks.
- `[monitor]` Persistent yellow tinge despite home phototherapy or repeated bili checks. Most physiologic jaundice resolves by week 2; persistence beyond 2 weeks (or rising bilirubin) needs evaluation for breast-milk jaundice vs. pathologic causes (e.g., biliary atresia)[2].
- `[call within 24h]` Pus, redness extending >1 cm around umbilicus, or foul odor (omphalitis is rare but emergent). Forceful projectile vomiting after feeds (rule out pyloric stenosis — typically presents week 3–6).
- `[call now]` Fever ≥100.4°F rectal. Lethargy, persistent inability to wake for feeds, color changes (blue/dusky around lips).

## Action items for parents

- **Confirm first-week weight check** has happened or is scheduled. Pediatrician wants to see weight regain trajectory.
- **Order birth certificate** if you haven't, and **apply for Social Security Number** (form SS-5 if not done in hospital). You need the SSN to open the 529 plan and claim the dependent on taxes.
- **Add baby to health insurance** within 30 days of birth (qualifying life event). This is a hard deadline — don't miss it.
- **Order more postpartum supplies as needed**: another 4–6 weeks of pads is realistic; nipple cream finishes faster than expected; the burp-cloth supply from registry is probably already short.
- **Establish night-shift split** if both parents are home. The "one parent does the 11pm–3am, the other does the 3am–7am" model is more sustainable than "both parents up for everything." See `mom/postpartum-physical-recovery.md`.
- **Begin (or continue) a feeding log** if breastfeeding — note feed start time, side, duration, output. A simple notes app or Huckleberry/Baby Tracker app is fine. Pediatrician will ask at the 1-mo visit.

## Enrichment opportunities

- **Tummy time** on parent's chest, several times daily, accumulating 5–10 minutes total. Counter-positions the natural side-preference that develops when infants spend most time supine[3].
- **Narrate ordinary tasks** during awake-alert windows: "I'm warming the bottle. The water is hot. The bottle is in the warmer." Conversational turns — not raw word count — predict later language outcomes (Romeo et al. 2018)[4].
- **Continue daily read-aloud** (5–15 min). Adult-directed material is fine; the prosody is the active ingredient.

## What's coming next

- **Week 03**: First common growth spurt; expect increased feeding frequency for 48h.
- **Week 04**: 1-month well visit window opens (typically scheduled day 28–35). Social smiles begin.
- **Week 05**: Peak crying / colic curve peaks ~6 weeks corrected age.
