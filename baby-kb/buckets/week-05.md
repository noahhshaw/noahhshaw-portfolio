# Week 05 — Days 28–34

## Focus
You're near the peak of the "PURPLE crying" curve (Period of crying that peaks ~6 weeks corrected age, Unexpected, Resists soothing, Pain-like face, Long lasting, Evening clustering)[1]. Inconsolable evenings are normal; documented across cultures and feeding methods. This is also when colic, defined by Wessel as ≥3h/day, ≥3 days/week, ≥3 weeks, becomes diagnosable.

## Expected developmental milestones
- **Motor**: head control improving in tummy time (10–20 sec sustained lifts).
- **Cognitive/sensory**: tracks objects 180° across visual field. Quiets to familiar voice.
- **Social**: social smiles consolidating. Beginning to make eye contact during feeds and conversations.
- **Language**: vowel sounds ("ah," "eh") emerging.
- **Feeding**: hunger/satiety cues sharpening — turning toward breast/bottle, opening mouth wide, settling visibly when full.

## Watch-fors
- `[low concern]` Inconsolable crying in evenings with no clear cause. PURPLE crying is not pathological. The 5 S's (swaddle, side/stomach hold, shush, swing, suck) help most infants reach a calmer state — see `topics/sleep-newborn-fundamentals.md`[2].
- `[monitor]` Forceful arching during/after feeds, frequent painful spit-up, refusal to feed despite hunger cues — possible GERD or cow's-milk-protein intolerance. Discuss at the 1-month or 2-month visit.
- `[call within 24h]` Bloody stool (mucus + blood streaks) suggests CMPI or anal fissure — pediatrician will distinguish. Sustained refusal of feeds.
- `[call now]` Fever ≥100.4°F rectal. Apnea or color change. Inconsolable crying with high-pitched scream + lethargy + bulging fontanelle (rule out meningitis).
- **Shaken-baby risk window**: peak crying coincides with peak risk of caregiver-induced traumatic injury. If you feel the urge to shake the baby, put him down in the crib (safe), step away for 10 minutes, call your partner or a friend. This is the single most-preventable cause of infant traumatic brain injury[3].

## Action items for parents
- **Confirm 1-month visit complete** with growth/feeding tracking.
- **Pre-schedule 2-month visit** (typical window day 56–70). Vaccines will be: HepB #2, RV #1, DTaP #1, Hib #1, PCV #1, IPV #1.
- **Daycare tours / nanny interviews** active now if return to work is in 7–11 weeks.
- **Mother**: pre-postpartum-visit notes. ACOG recommends EPDS rescreening at the 6-week visit. If you're scoring >10 between visits, message the OB.

## Enrichment opportunities
- **Tummy time** 20–30 minutes/day cumulative. Drape across your forearm in football hold for variation.
- **Read aloud** 15 minutes minimum/day. Begin reading the same 3–5 books on rotation — repetition supports rhythm/predictability recognition.
- **Music**: live singing during diaper changes and bath time. The serve-and-return cadence is the active ingredient, not the song quality.
- **Bilingual minority-language exposure**: if planned, the second-language parent should be at "always second language with the baby" by now.

## What's coming next
- **Week 06**: Mother's 6-week postpartum visit — clearance for exercise/sex typical.
- **Week 07**: Crying begins to taper. Plan 2-month vaccines.
- **Week 08**: 2-month well visit window opens.
